# Pre-registration: is the companion of TIC 71945261 too big for its Gaia mass?

Written 2026-11-18 (about 23:40 UTC), **before** job gaia-tic71945261-v1 ran. By MV2 of
Munim, Inc.; made with AI agents working in his name, and not yet checked by a human expert.

## Where it came from
The outlier-proof SB1 transit search (sb1_robust 0.2.0, pre-registered in
`findings/2026-11-18-sb1-robust-prereg.md`) found one clean, deep eclipse at the period of a
Gaia DR3 SB1 orbit, in chunk 7 of 8 (`data/sb1rob-v1/k7/`):

| | value |
|---|---|
| Gaia DR3 | 6853008555751131648 (TYC 6340-23-1, TIC 71945261, G = 10.53, ϖ = 9.02 mas) |
| Gaia SB1 orbit | P = 2.32893 d, K = 16.16 km/s, e = 0.040 |
| Primary (Gaia FLAME) | M₁ = 0.845 M☉, R₁ = 0.927 R☉ |
| Minimum companion mass | m₂ sin i = 0.0965 M☉ (a very-low-mass star near the hydrogen-burning limit) |
| TESS, sector 92 | box depth 4.98 % at the Gaia period, S/N 124; flat-bottomed dip ~6 % deep, ~2.6 h long |
| Depth expected for a normal companion of that mass | 1.73 % (R₂ ≈ 0.12 R☉) |

The plot is `data/sb1rob-v1/k7/plots/6853008555751131648_s92.png`. Taken at face value, the
depth gives k = R₂/R₁ ≈ 0.22–0.25 and R₂ ≈ 0.21–0.23 R☉. That is about 70–85 % larger than a
main-sequence star of 0.097 M☉. Because the system eclipses, sin i ≈ 1, so the companion mass
cannot hide behind the inclination.

## What the job does
`eblm_single.py` 0.1.0 runs the **unchanged** `eblm_fit` 0.1.0 (commit 10b5aeb, the code that
fit 70 Gaia EBLMs in `findings/2026-11-18-eblm-fit-results.md`) on all TESS sectors (up to 8):
- the eclipse shape with the Gaia period, which gives k, b and a/R₁;
- R₁ from the light-curve density and Kepler's law, compared with FLAME (`r1_ratio`);
- the ellipsoidal amplitude, compared with the amplitude Gaia's mass predicts;
- the occultation depth, which gives the companion's temperature;
- odd/even depths.

TOI-1251.01 (Gaia DR3 2160710158704303360, m₂ sin i = 0.091 M☉, box depth 0.63 %, which looks
normal) runs in the same job as a comparison. Both systems are labelled by `outcome()`, which
was fixed before the job ran:

| Label | eblm_fit verdict | What it would mean |
|---|---|---|
| A_COMPANION_HEAVIER_THAN_GAIA | MASS_TENSION_HEAVIER or TOO_LUMINOUS | The eclipse is real but Gaia's K (and so its mass) is too low. Evidence for LEAD 2 that a correct DR3 SB1 period can come with a wrong amplitude. |
| A2_COMPANION_LIGHTER_THAN_GAIA | MASS_TENSION_LIGHTER | The ellipsoidal signal is weaker than the Gaia mass predicts. |
| B_OVERSIZED_COMPANION | (BD_)EBLM_LARGE_RADIUS (R₂ > 1.3 × main sequence) **and** 0.8 ≤ r1_ratio ≤ 1.25 | A ~0.1 M☉ star far larger than models allow, with the ellipsoidal and temperature checks passed. Youth or an undetected mass error are the first explanations to test. |
| B0_LARGE_BUT_PRIMARY_SCALE_DISAGREES | LARGE_RADIUS with r1_ratio outside 0.8–1.25 | The radius scale is unreliable. No claim. |
| C_NORMAL_COMPANION | EBLM_CONSISTENT / EBLM_SMALL_RADIUS | A normal EBLM. The box depth overstated the eclipse. |
| D_TWIN_AT_2P | TWIN_AT_2P | Two similar stars at twice the period, so the Gaia SB1 solution is wrong. |
| E_NO_ECLIPSE | NO_ECLIPSE | The sb1_robust hit was not real. |
| G_GRAZING_RADIUS_UNMEASURED | (BD_)EBLM_GRAZING | Radius not measurable. No claim. |
| UNTESTED | NO_TIC / NO_TESS_DATA / ERROR | Fix and re-run. |

Predictions written down now:
- **TIC 71945261:** we expect **A or B**. The depth is too large for C unless the box depth
  is badly biased.
  - A normal 0.097 M☉ companion gives an ellipsoidal amplitude of about 300 ppm (our
    estimate). A 0.25 M☉ companion would give about 800 ppm.
- **TOI-1251.01:** we expect **C**. If it comes out A or B as well, the pipeline is suspect
  and TIC 71945261's label is not interpreted.

  stop.
- Plots are opened before any label is reported.

  to AAVSO VSX as a new EA variable, credited to MV2 of Munim, Inc. with the AI disclosure.

  kinematic youth check (young moving groups) and a second-epoch RV from the literature or
