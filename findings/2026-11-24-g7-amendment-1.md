# Step g7, amendment 1: the fallback ephemerides were broken for four of seven stars; fixed before Gaia DR4

MV2, Munim, Inc. Made with AI agents working in his name. Text and data CC BY 4.0.
Written 2026-11-24 (~07:30 UTC), eight days before Gaia DR4 (2 Dec 2026, 12:00 CET). No DR4 data
exist yet, and none of the queued jobs (sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1, sb2-ebfit-v1) has run, so nothing
in this amendment was chosen after seeing a result it grades.

## What we checked

Step g7 (`findings/2026-11-24-g7-prereg.md`) fits Gaia DR4's per-transit double-lined velocities
with the eclipse timing fixed. Its rule for the timing was: use the queued TESS eclipse-timing job
if it passes its controls, **else the SPOC multi-sector TCE on exo.MAST**. If the timing job has
not run by DR4 day, that fallback decides g7. Following our rule *test the control rows first*, we
read the SPOC TCE of every g7 star (exo.MAST DV headers, `data/g7-ephemerides/ephemeris_check.csv`).

| star | role | SPOC TCE | usable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| HD 250735 | target | s0002-s0072, P/2, converged, 22 events | yes |
| CD-28 7251 | target | s0062 only, P/2, **fit not converged** | no |
| TYC 3616-2395-1 | target | s0057 only, P/2, converged, 14 events | yes (single sector) |
| ZZ Boo | control | **wrong period** (6.5075 d; true 4.99177 d) | no |
| CW Eri | control | P/2, **not converged, NTRANS 0** | no |
| EY Cep | control | converged, 19 events; agrees with the literature to 0.0004 d | yes |
| BK Peg | control | P/2, **not converged, NTRANS 0** | no |

**As registered, the fallback would have failed three of the four controls**, and g7 would have
reported CONTROLS_FAIL for a reason that has nothing to do with Gaia. That is a broken check, not a
result, so we fix it now.

## Amended rule (fixed now)

1. **Timing job first, unchanged:** if sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1 ended CONTROLS_OK, its ephemeris is
   used for the three targets.
2. **Controls always use their published linear ephemerides** (primary minimum, BJD_TDB), which
   are independent of Gaia and better than any single TESS fit:
   ZZ Boo `2458942.300641(7) + 4.991765196(61)E` (Southworth 2023, arXiv:2212.02595);
   CW Eri `2452500.37624(69) + 2.72837024(27)E` (Overall & Southworth 2024, arXiv:2401.13397);
   EY Cep `2459944.403889(14) + 7.97148361(20)E` (Southworth 2026, arXiv:2607.10701);
   BK Peg `2450706.46975(20) + 5.48991130(12)E` (Kutluay & Southworth 2026, arXiv:2603.20872).
   t_conj is moved to the cycle nearest the middle of the DR4 time span (BTJD ≈ 866), and
   eph_sigma = max(0.001 d, propagated error).
3. **A target's TCE is valid** only if CONVRGE = 1, NTRANS ≥ 3, and TPERIOD or 2·TPERIOD is within
   0.1% of Gaia's period. For a valid TCE, eph_sigma = max(0.005 d, the largest offset of any
   single-sector TCE epoch from the adopted ephemeris, and the drift that the TESS-Gaia period
   difference builds up between the TCE epoch and the DR4 mid-point).
   - HD 250735: multi-sector TCE; the single-sector epochs sit at −0.0005 d (S43), **−0.0225 d
     (S71)** and −0.0002 d (S72) from it, so eph_sigma = 0.023 d (was 0.005).
   - TYC 3616-2395-1: S57 TCE for t_conj, Gaia's P; 2·TPERIOD differs from Gaia's P by
     0.000126 d over 525 cycles, so eph_sigma = 0.066 d.
4. **No valid TESS ephemeris (CD-28 7251):** t_conj comes from Gaia DR3's own orbit (BTJD 393.670,
   eph_sigma 0.19 d), so the conjunction grade C is **not graded** (it would test Gaia against
   itself). K and the masses are still fitted; the scan (±0.05 P = ±0.35 d) covers the uncertainty.

The filled inputs are in `runbook/g7/targets.json` (this commit). Inclinations stay FILL, as before.

## What this costs, said plainly

With eph_sigma 0.023 d, the HD 250735 C grade needs |dt| > 3·sqrt(σ_dt² + 0.023²) ≥ 0.069 d to say
CONJ_EARLY. The DR3 hint we registered g7 partly to test is 0.020 d. **On the fallback ephemeris, g7
can no longer detect it**; only the timing job's ephemeris (or DR4 epoch photometry) can. We keep the
registered 60/35/5 prediction unchanged and note that, on the fallback, CONJ_EARLY has become much
harder to reach.

## An exploratory note on HD 250735 (not a result)

SPOC's single-sector fits of two *consecutive* sectors disagree by 0.022 d (32 min) in eclipse
phase: S71's epoch falls 0.0225 d before the three-sector ephemeris, S72's falls on it. S43's
single-sector period (2.94462 d) is also 0.11% short, which over that sector's eight events amounts
to a similar 0.023 d drift. These are planet-model fits to V-shaped, nearly grazing eclipses (fitted
impact 0.78-0.97). The fitted epoch of such a fit can move when the model fits badly, so we read this
as a warning about the TCE ephemeris, not as timing variations. **Added prediction (fixed now,
before any light curve is timed):** when sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1 times every eclipse, the S71 and S72
eclipses will lie on one linear ephemeris within 0.005 d, i.e. the S71 offset is a fit artefact: 75%.

## An exploratory note on CD-28 7251: Gaia's period holds, the TESS pipeline's does not

The S62 TCE's period (2 × 3.479165 = 6.95833 d) differs from Gaia's spectroscopic 6.96109 ± 0.00015 d
by 0.0028 d. Which is right matters for every fold. Gaia's two faint G-band epochs (BTJD 233.384 and
240.388, `data/sb2-new-ebs/cd-28-7251_gaia_dr3_epoch_g.csv`) are 396 and 395 cycles before the TCE epoch
(BTJD 2989.880). With Gaia's period they land 0.095 d and 0.138 d before the extrapolated TCE eclipse,
which is inside an eclipse of about 6 h. With the TCE's period they would miss it by about 1.0 d. Taking
the TCE epoch as good to ±0.3 d (a fold at P/2 mixes two eclipses that are 0.32 d off half phase), the
2015-2023 link gives P ≈ 6.9609 ± 0.0008 d. That agrees with Gaia (0.3σ) and not with the TCE (3σ). The
cycle count is unambiguous: 395 or 397 cycles would need P ≈ 6.979 or 6.943 d. We read this as the SPOC
fit at exactly P/2 being biased by the displaced secondary eclipse, which is why the fallback uses
Gaia's P. This is a hand calculation, done after the dips had been seen, and it is not a grade.

## The other queued jobs checked for the same flaw

- **sb2-ebfit-v1** does not use TCEs. It finds the eclipse phase by its own fold search within Gaia's
  period ± max(10σ, 3×10⁻⁴ P). For each control, the published period lies inside that window: ZZ Boo
  is 1.2×10⁻⁴ off with a window of ±1.5×10⁻³; CW Eri 4×10⁻⁵ (±8×10⁻⁴); EY Cep 2.6×10⁻⁴ (±2.4×10⁻³);
  BK Peg 8.9×10⁻⁴ (±1.6×10⁻³). For the targets, the TESS-based periods of HD 250735 and TYC 3616-2395-1
  are 1.7×10⁻⁴ and 1.3×10⁻⁴ from Gaia's, and CD-28 7251's link above is 2×10⁻⁴ from it. No amendment
  is needed.
- **sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1** finds eclipses with no ephemeris and groups them on a period grid of
  ±6σ of Gaia's period. It uses no TCE.

## Sanity checks of the literature ephemerides against TESS (hand arithmetic)

- EY Cep: the published ephemeris predicts the SPOC TCE epoch to −0.0004 d.
- BK Peg: the non-converged P/2 TCE epoch sits at half phase, 0.0097 d early. The published
  e cos ω = −0.00356 displaces the secondary eclipse by (2P/π) e cos ω = −0.012 d. These agree.
- CW Eri's TCE (NTRANS 0) and ZZ Boo's (wrong period) cannot be compared.

## Limits

The literature ephemerides are linear. EY Cep may have a light-time effect, but its timing r.m.s.
is 3.6 s, far below eph_sigma. Gaia's e and ω are still used for the fit shape, as registered. The
conjunction time is taken equal to the mid-eclipse time. All arithmetic in this note was done by
hand (no code ran) and is repeated by `g7_run.py` on DR4 day. This work was carried out with AI
agents, and no human expert has checked it yet.
