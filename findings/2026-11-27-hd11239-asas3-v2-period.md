# HD 11239 in ASAS-3: Gaia's period holds, the sb2-ebfit-v2 TESS period does not

MV2, Munim, Inc. Made with AI agents working in MV2's name; no human expert has checked it.
Text CC BY 4.0; data table CC BY 4.0 (ASAS-3 photometry: Pojmanski 2002, Acta Astron. 52, 397).
Written 2026-11-27, 20:20-20:50 UTC. Data: data/hd11239-asas3-v2period/hd11239_asas3_faint_v2.csv.

## What this adds, and what it does not
ASAS-3 has already confirmed HD 11239's eclipses on Gaia's ephemeris: release v0.75.0 (26 Nov 2026,
findings/2026-11-26-hd11239-asas3.md) found 20 of 22 faint points within 0.06 of an orbit of Gaia
DR3's conjunctions. This note does not repeat that claim. It answers a question that came up a day
later: the pre-registered joint TESS fit sb2-ebfit-v2 (findings/2026-11-27-sb2-ebfit-v2-graded.md,
label FIT_DOUBTFUL) gave P = 3.119557 +/- 0.000023 d, 28 sigma below Gaia DR3's 3.1208264 +/- 0.000039 d,
and the queued TESS timing test (findings/2026-11-27-hd11239-timing-prereg.md) treats a real period
or timing shift (T2') as possible. Over the 1,377-2,222 orbits between the ASAS-3 years (2000-2009)
and the v2 fit's reference time, the two periods differ by 1.75-2.8 days (0.56-0.90 of an orbit), so
ASAS-3 alone says which one is right. No TESS data were looked at; the frozen TESS test is untouched.

## Rule (written before any phase was computed)
Same data rule as v0.75.0, plus a second ephemeris. Honest note: four very faint magnitudes were
visible in the first fetch before the rule was written, but no phases had been computed.
1. ASAS-3 star 014936-3944.5, dataset 3 star 1 (field F0200-40_125), grades A and B, MAG_3 < 29.
2. Faint: MAG_3 >= median + 0.07 mag.
3. Test G, Gaia DR3 ephemeris: conjunctions BTJD 389.2946 and 390.8619 + n x 3.1208263847 d
   (runbook/hd11239-timing-v1/targets.json). Test V, v2 TESS-fit ephemeris: BTJD 2126.0435053 and
   2126.0435053 + P/2, + n x 3.119557085 d (the "alt" ephemeris in the same file). BTJD = HJD - 2457000
   (the HJD-UTC vs BJD-TDB difference, about 1 minute, is ignored).
4. In-window: |offset| <= 0.06 of an orbit from either conjunction (two windows = 0.24 of the orbit).
5. Pass: binomial P(k >= k_obs | n, 0.24) < 1e-4 AND >= 60% of faint points in-window.
6. Prior: G passes 70%; V fails 85%.

## Data
The light curve was read from http://www.astrouw.edu.pl/cgi-asas/asas_cgi_get_data?014936-3944.5,asas3
on 2026-11-27 with a text-extraction tool (no shell was available in this run). 522 grade-A/B points
were extracted (v0.75.0's script read 537 directly from the file, so about 15 lines were missed). The
median, counted by hand, is 9.727 (253 points below, 11 equal, 258 above), the same value v0.75.0 found.
Threshold 9.797. A second extraction that asked only for points at or above 9.797 returned exactly the
same 21 grade-A/B points, with errors and frame numbers (one grade-D point, 4164.51288 at 9.834, is
excluded by rule 1). v0.75.0 had 22; the missing one is among the ~15 unextracted lines.

## Result
| Test | ephemeris | in-window | expected by chance | binomial p | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | Gaia DR3, P = 3.1208264 d | **19 of 21** (90%) | 5.0 | 2.1e-10 | PASS |
| V | sb2-ebfit-v2, P = 3.1195571 d | **1 of 21** (5%) | 5.0 | 0.997 (k >= 1) | FAIL |

Both predictions held. Under Gaia's ephemeris 15 of the 21 points lie within 0.017 of an orbit of a
conjunction: 8 at conjunction 0 and 7 at conjunction 1, plus 4 more within 0.06. Under the v2
ephemeris the same points scatter over the whole orbit, and the only in-window point (HJD 2454722.80)
is one of the two that fall outside Gaia's windows.

Exploratory (not graded): the 15 tight points have a mean offset of -0.0069 orbits (-0.021 d, about
30 minutes early) with no visible trend over 825-1,665 orbits. If this is entirely due to the period,
it implies P about 3.120843 d, +1.7e-5 d from Gaia's value (0.4 sigma). The period scan in the first
draft of this rule (step 1e-6 d) was not run because this run had no shell; it is left to a job.

## What it means for the queued TESS test
The 2000-2009 eclipses, Gaia's 2014-2017 orbit and TESS sector 30 (2020; the v2 fit put its eclipse
0.002 d from Gaia's prediction) all agree with Gaia's period. A period of 3.119557 d would have put the
ASAS-3 eclipses 0.56-0.90 of an orbit away from where they are. So the v2 period is almost certainly a
fit failure (a wrong local minimum) and not a real change. This makes T2 (sector 3 on Gaia's
ephemeris) much more likely than T2' (a shift of +0.25 to +0.35 d). The TESS test stays frozen and will
be graded as written. Its decision rule for T2 already applies: re-fit HD 11239's masses with a period
scan starting at Gaia's P.

## Limits
- The data were extracted by a text tool, not parsed by code; 15 of 537 A/B lines were missed. The
  faint list was checked by a second, independent extraction.
- ASAS-3's 15-arcsecond pixels blend nearby stars; it confirms timing, not depth. Depths of the
  in-window points range from 0.07 to 1.07 mag; v0.75.0 judged five points 0.23-1.07 mag below
  baseline (including 4455.70, 1.07 mag) as probably bad frames, and that question is not revisited here.
- The rule was written in this run's work folder before phases were computed, but it was not
  published before the data were fetched.
