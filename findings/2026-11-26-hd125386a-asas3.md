# HD 125386A: a new eclipsing Am-star binary found by Gaia alone, confirmed in ASAS-3 (2000-2009)

## The star
HD 125386A = Gaia DR3 5854558019550047872 = TYC 9006-2161-1 = CD-61 4343A = TIC 1021067547
(TIC 337318750 is the blended AB entry) = ASAS 142119-6138.9. RA 215.32962, Dec -61.64882 (J2000).
SIMBAD: HD 125386 is a visual pair (WDS J14213-6139AB, NZO 49), spectral type **A2mA5-A6 (an Am
star)**, with B 2.4 arcsec east (Gaia DR3 5854558023859109760). Gaia G 9.49, BP-RP 0.29; TIC Teff
9,670 K, distance 279 pc.

Gaia DR3 single-lined orbit: P = 3.6734169 +- 0.0001682 d, K1 = 48.48 +- 1.11 km/s, e = 0.016 +-
0.020 (consistent with circular), f(m) = 0.0433 Msun. For a ~2 Msun A star that means a companion
of at least ~0.68 Msun (a K dwarf if on the main sequence).

## How it was found
The pre-registered blind search of all 7,978 short-period Gaia DR3 SB1 orbits (job g8-dr3-sb1-v1,
findings/2026-11-26-g8-dr3-sb1-results.md) flagged it: 4 of 83 Gaia G epochs are 0.04-0.20 mag
faint, **all four at conjunction 0** (the visible star behind its companion; dphase +0.013,
+0.017, -0.013, -0.003), none at conjunction 1, none elsewhere. Gaia does not call it eclipsing;
VSX lists it only as a Gaia DR3 "VAR" with a spurious 0.0247 d period. Not in Prsa et al. 2022,
IJspeert et al. 2021 or 2024, TESS Ten Thousand (any table), Eclipses by Appointment
(accepted-systems.csv at 6699c5f, searched by RA and Dec), the ASAS-SN variable catalogue,
ZTF periodic variables, ATLAS variables, ACVS or GCVS/NSV (VizieR CfA cones of 5-10 arcsec); no TOI
or CTOI on ExoFOP for either TIC; a web search for "HD 125386" finds nothing on it.

## ASAS-3 test (as pre-written)
ASAS-3 V, series 142119-6138.9 (field F1400-64_050), aperture MAG_3, grades A/B: **526 points**,
HJD 2451907.8-2455088.5, i.e. 627-1,492 orbits before Gaia's conjunction 0. None is fainter than
median + 0.40, so none is dropped. Median V = 9.0985 (ASAS's 15-arcsec pixels blend A and B, so
the eclipse is diluted). Faint threshold 9.1685: **22 faint points**.

Gaia's conjunction 0: T0 = Gaia 2192.44204 = HJD 2457389.94204 (mean anomaly 147.644 deg).

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Faint points with abs(phi) <= 0.12 of Gaia's conjunction 0 (chance 0.24) | **18 of 22**, on 18 different nights, 2002-2009 |
| One-sided binomial p | **1.8e-8** (pass threshold 1e-4) |
| Largest abs(phi) of the 18 | 0.033 (all 18 within -0.033 and -0.0004) |
| Median phi of the 18 | -0.0139 (about 74 minutes early) |
| The 4 others | phi -0.175, -0.179, +0.336, -0.421 |

**The test passes.** The faint ASAS-3 points sit in a band only 0.033 of an orbit wide, at the
conjunction where Gaia's velocities put the A star behind its companion, carried back up to 1,492
orbits. The 18 in-window depths are 0.07-0.14 mag in the blended V light.

Not graded (reported as pre-registered): a through-origin fit of O-C against cycle number for the
18 in-window points gives a period correction of **+5.8e-5 d (0.34 sigma of Gaia's period
error)**, i.e. P ~ 3.67347 d; the early-on scatter of +-0.09 d matches the eclipse width expected
for this pair (~0.18 d). One of the four out-of-window points (phi -0.421) lies within 0.1 of
phase 0.5; we do not claim a secondary eclipse.

Not graded, checked after the test: the same star in ASAS-3 field F1500-64_051 (183 rows) has 6
faint A/B points on nights not in the main series; 4 lie within 0.071 of conjunction 0 (phi
-0.014, -0.037, -0.005, -0.071), and 2 near phase 0.5 (+0.485, -0.472) that same-night points of the
main series do not confirm. A third field (F1424-56_078, 21 rows) has one faint point, at phi
-0.026. The first block of the ASAS file (142117-6138.6, 47 rows, scattered 9.1-9.8) is another
designation and was not used.

## What it is
An Am star of ~2 Msun eclipsed every 3.673 days by a companion of >= 0.68 Msun, found from Gaia's
own photometry and velocities alone and confirmed with independent photometry from a decade
earlier. Only the primary eclipse is seen (Gaia: no dip at conjunction 1), as expected when a
cooler, much fainter companion passes in front of the A star. Eclipsing Am stars with a measured
single-lined orbit are useful for testing Am-star rotation and synchronisation in close binaries;
with TESS photometry the inclination, radii and, from K1 and the mass function, the companion's
mass follow.

## Limits
- Work carried out with AI agents; no human expert has checked it.
- The rule was written before the data were fetched but published only with the result.
- ASAS-3 blends A and B (2.4 arcsec), so depth and shape are not measured; Gaia resolves the pair,
  and Gaia's own dips are in A's photometry.
- "New" = absent from every catalogue and project listed above on 26 Nov 2026. VSX lists only a
  Gaia "VAR" with a wrong period.
- Not yet submitted to VSX (no AAVSO account) or checked in TESS photometry.
