## The star
TYC 1316-1554-1 = Gaia DR3 3350075697770930304 = ASAS 055236+1741.3, RA 88.149251, Dec +17.688585.
Gaia G 10.58, BP-RP 0.72 (F/G type), parallax 2.90 mas (~345 pc). Gaia DR3 single-lined orbit:
P = 1.6262843 +- 0.0000141 d, e = 0.078 +- 0.010, K1 = 95.2 +- 1.0 km/s, f(m) = 0.144 Msun, so the
unseen companion is at least ~0.85 Msun for a ~1.2 Msun primary. It is one of the six flags of the
blind Gaia SB1 search (findings/2026-11-26-g8-dr3-sb1-results.md) that no catalogue lists as
eclipsing: five faint Gaia G epochs, three at conjunction 0 (0.03-0.10 mag) and two at
conjunction 1 (0.03-0.04 mag), from five different orbits.

## Ephemeris from Gaia alone
Conjunction 0 (visible star behind): T0 = Gaia 2192.12038 = HJD 2457389.62038. Conjunction 1 at
phase 0.45582 after it (the orbit is slightly eccentric, e cos omega = -0.069).

## ASAS-3 test (as pre-written)
ASAS-3 V, dataset 1 of 055236+1741.3 (field F0552+16_434), aperture MAG_2, grades A/B: **332
points**, HJD 2452621.7-2455165.7, i.e. 1,367-2,932 orbits before Gaia's T0 (Gaia's period
error alone gives a phase uncertainty of 0.012-0.026 over that span). None is dropped as bad.
Baseline (median) V = 10.686, MAD = 0.024 mag, sigma = 0.036, so the faint threshold is
baseline + 3 sigma = 10.793 (the 0.07 floor does not apply). **6 faint points.**

- In the two windows (|phi| <= 0.08 at either conjunction; chance 0.32): **3 of 6**, all at
  conjunction 0. One-sided binomial **p = 0.29**. The pass rule needed p < 1e-4.
- **The test does not pass.** With only six faint points at this star's brightness (ASAS-3 noise
  0.036 mag against a ~0.1 mag eclipse), ASAS-3 cannot confirm or rule out the eclipses. We do
  not count TYC 1316-1554-1 as confirmed.
- Pre-registered, not graded: the three conjunction-0 points give a period correction of
  -2.9e-5 d (-2.1 sigma of Gaia's period error).

## Not pre-registered (vetting after the test; read with care)
- All six faint points lie between phi -0.024 and +0.137, i.e. on the same side of the orbit, just
  after Gaia's conjunction 0, and the three oldest (2004-2006, 2,188-2,461 cycles back) are the
  furthest off (+0.10 to +0.14). A through-origin fit of O-C against cycle for all six gives a
  period 6.4e-5 d shorter than Gaia's (4.5 sigma of Gaia's quoted error), which would put all
  six within ~0.09 of conjunction 0. With a 1.63 d period an eclipse lasts ~0.1 of an orbit, so
  this is what a slightly-too-long Gaia period would look like, but it is a pattern found after
  looking and it rests on six points.
- The 3387.557 dip is also seen in the independent reduction of the same night in ASAS field
  F0552+24_479 (dataset 3: V 10.796 against a baseline near 10.69).
- Next: a pre-registered TESS test (job sb1-new-ebs-tess-v1) that measures the eclipse epoch and
  period directly; if the eclipses are real, TESS (2021-2025, close to Gaia's epoch) decides it.
