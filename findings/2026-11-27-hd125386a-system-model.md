## Background

HD 125386A (Gaia DR3 5854558019550047872, TIC 1021067547, G = 9.49, A2mA5-A6) was found to eclipse
by MV2's blind search of Gaia DR3 single-lined orbits (`findings/2026-11-26-g8-dr3-sb1-results.md`)
and confirmed in ASAS-3 photometry from 2000-2009 phased only on Gaia's orbit: 18 of 22 faint
points within 0.033 of an orbit of the conjunction at which the A star is behind its companion
(`findings/2026-11-26-hd125386a-asas3.md`). Only that one eclipse is seen.

Re-checked 27 Nov 2026 (11:15-11:30 UTC): VSX by coordinates (only the Gaia DR3 "VAR" entry with
period 0.0247 d), VizieR CfA cones of TESS Ten Thousand tables 2-4, ASAS-SN variables, IJspeert
et al. 2024 and GCVS/NSV (no rows, 20 arcsec), ExoFOP (no TOI/CTOI), Eclipses by Appointment
commits through 18f41dc (26 Nov 19:55 UTC; none names it), web searches for HD 125386, SAO 252714
and CD-61 4343: no record of eclipses or of a spectroscopic orbit outside Gaia DR3.

## Gaia orbit

P = 3.67341686 ± 0.00016819 d, K1 = 48.48 ± 1.11 km/s, e = 0.016 ± 0.020 (consistent with
circular, as tides predict at 3.7 d). Mass function

f(m) = 1.0361e-7 (1 - e^2)^1.5 K1^3 P = **0.0433 ± 0.0030 Msun**.

Gaia ESP-HS gives Teff 8860 K (TIC 8.2: 9669 K); GSP-Phot and FLAME give nothing. We therefore
bracket the A star with three main-sequence scenarios and fix the companion radius at 0.65 Rsun:

| scenario | M1 (Msun) | R1 (Rsun) | M2 at i = 90 (Msun) | a (Rsun) | eclipses need i > | central duration (d) | primary depth, central, no limb darkening (mag) | synchronous v_eq (km/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low  | 1.7 | 1.6 | 0.615 | 13.25 | 80.2 deg | 0.199 | 0.196 | 22.0 |
| mid  | 1.9 | 1.8 | 0.657 | 13.70 | 79.7 deg | 0.210 | 0.152 | 24.8 |
| high | 2.1 | 2.0 | 0.697 | 14.12 | 79.2 deg | 0.221 | 0.121 | 27.5 |

Because the system eclipses, i > 79 deg, so sin^3 i > 0.95 and the companion mass is pinned to
within 2% of the i = 90 value for a given M1: **M2 = 0.62-0.70 Msun**, i.e. a K5-K7 dwarf
(K1 error adds about ±3%). A white dwarf of this mass would give a transit of under 0.01% depth,
not the 0.04-0.20 mag dips Gaia saw, so the companion is a main-sequence K dwarf.

Consistency with what was already seen:
- The central eclipse lasts 0.054-0.060 of an orbit (half-width 0.027-0.030); the ASAS-3 faint
  points cluster within 0.033 of the conjunction. Consistent.
- Expected primary depth 0.12-0.20 mag for a central eclipse; Gaia's four faint epochs are
  0.04-0.20 mag. Consistent.
- Secondary eclipse (K dwarf hidden): light ratio in V about 0.006 (black bodies, 9000 K vs 4400 K),
  i.e. a 0.006 mag dip, far below ASAS-3's 0.036 mag noise. That is why only one eclipse is seen.
- Synchronous rotation 22-28 km/s, slow enough for an Am star (Am stars rotate below about
  100 km/s). A spectroscopic v sin i near this value would show the A star is tidally synchronised.

## Pre-registered TESS predictions (frozen 2026-11-27, before any TESS data are seen)

Reference conjunction (A star behind): Gaia time 2192.44204 = BTJD 389.94204 (BTJD = Gaia time -
1802.5), period 3.67341686 d. N = cycles from the reference; sigma_P = 0.00016819 d.

- **T1 timing.** In every TESS sector with usable data, primary eclipses fall within
  |dt| <= 3 N sigma_P + 0.10 d of the Gaia ephemeris.
- **T2 duration.** Primary eclipse total duration (first to last contact) between 0.10 and 0.23 d.
- **T3 secondary.** A secondary eclipse at phase 0.50 ± 0.02, with secondary/primary depth ratio in
  the TESS band between 0.04 and 0.20 (black-body model 0.07-0.15 for a central eclipse; this ratio
  does not depend on blending, so the strong contamination in TESS pixels does not affect it).
- **T4 quiet outside eclipse.** Semi-amplitude of any variation at P or P/2 outside eclipses
  below 0.5% of the star's flux (model ellipsoidal effect 0.08%).
- **T5 depth.** In crowding-corrected flux (SPOC PDCSAP or equivalent), primary depth between 8% and
  22% of the flux. TIC 8.2 lists a contamination ratio of 1.77 (the visual companion B is 2.4 arcsec
  away), so uncorrected light curves will show shallower eclipses; T5 is graded only on
  crowding-corrected flux and is void if none exists.

Decision rules: T1+T2 pass -> the TESS light curve confirms the Gaia eclipse timing independently of
ASAS-3. T3 ratio > 0.3 -> the companion is hotter than a K dwarf (or the eclipsed light comes from a
third star) and the mass model above is wrong. T4 fail -> spots or pulsation; still eclipsing.
If T1-T3 pass, a light-curve fit with K1 from Gaia gives i, R1/a and R2/a, and with the mass
function a dynamical estimate of both masses once a second velocity amplitude exists (DR4 epoch
RVs, 2 Dec 2026, may contain the companion's lines only if it contributes enough light; at 0.6% in
V it probably does not).

## Limits

The A-star mass and radius are assumed from its spectral type, not measured. Black-body surface
brightness ratios ignore limb and gravity darkening and real stellar spectra (expected error tens
of per cent on T3's model, which is why its window is wide). The ASAS-3 confirmation is a vetting
test whose rule was fixed before fetching but published with the result.
