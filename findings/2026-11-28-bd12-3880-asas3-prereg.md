# Pre-registration: ASAS-3 timing test of BD+12 3880 (Gaia DR3 4318964656978448768)

## The candidate

BD+12 3880 (TIC 300539729; RA 289.91559, Dec +12.35796; G 8.92) is a tier-2 candidate from the Gaia DR3 single-lined (SB1) search (findings/2026-11-27-sb1-tier2-prereg.md). Gaia DR3 orbit: P = 1.99580035 +/- 0.0000187 d, K1 = 59.68 +/- 0.42 km/s, e = 0.010 +/- 0.008, f(m) = 0.0440 Msun. Gaia's 67 G-band epochs show a double wave (maxima ~8.88 at quadratures, minima 8.949-8.953 at conj1 and 9.000 at conj0) plus seven unrejected epochs fainter than 8.98, all within 0.035 of an orbit of conj0. Our reading: ellipsoidal variation (~3%) plus a ~0.05 mag eclipse at conj0, the conjunction where the Gaia-measured star is behind its companion. A pre-registered TESS test (L1-L3) is queued (sb1-tier2-tess-v1) and has not run.

Conjunction times from the Gaia orbit (Gaia time = BJD - 2455197.5):
- conj0 = Gaia time 2190.5457 = BTJD 388.0457 (BJD 2457388.0457)
- conj1 = conj0 + 0.9961 d = BTJD 389.0418

## Why ASAS-3 can decide something here

ASAS-3 (Pojmanski 2002) observed Dec < +28 in V from 2000 to 2009. The star is at Dec +12.4 and V ~ 9.0, where ASAS-3 point errors are about 0.02-0.03 mag. The predicted dimming at conj0 relative to the median is ~0.08-0.09 mag (half the ellipsoidal amplitude plus the eclipse), i.e. about 3 times the point error, so a faint-point counting test has power (lesson from TYC 7628-1267-1, where a 0.04 mag eclipse gave an inconclusive test).

Period error carried back: the ASAS-3 epochs (HJD ~2451900-2455050, BTJD -5100 to -1950) are 1,170-2,750 orbits before conj0; 2,750 x 0.0000187 d = 0.051 d = 0.026 of an orbit. The conj0 time itself is uncertain by roughly 0.01-0.02 d (Gaia SB1 t_periastron error for a 2-day orbit). A window of +/-0.10 of an orbit covers both comfortably.

Known complication: V1208 Aql lies 61 arcsec away. ASAS-3 pixels are ~15 arcsec; apertures MAG_0..MAG_4 have diameters of 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 pixels (30-90 arcsec). We use MAG_1 (45 arcsec diameter, 22.5 arcsec radius) as the primary aperture so the neighbour's core is outside the aperture, and MAG_2 as a check. If SIMBAD shows V1208 Aql brighter than V = 8 we will add an amendment before fetching the light curve.

## Data

ASAS-3 light curve via www.astrouw.edu.pl/cgi-asas/asas_cgi_get_data?<id>,asas3 with id near 191940+1221.5 (neighbouring ddmm.m tried if empty). Points used: GRADE A or B only, MAG_1 not equal to 29.999 (missing). m_med = median MAG_1 of those points. A point is FAINT if MAG_1 >= m_med + 0.06.

Phases: reference epoch T_ref = conj0 - 2000 P = BTJD 388.0457 - 3991.6007 = BTJD -3603.5550 (HJD 2453396.4450). For a point at HJD t, phase = frac((t - 2457000 - T_ref)/P) with P = 1.99580035 d. HJD vs BJD and UTC vs TDB differences (< 0.002 d) are ignored (< 0.001 of an orbit).

Windows: W0 = phase within 0.10 of 0.000 (conj0); W1 = phase within 0.10 of 0.4991 (conj1); Q = phase within 0.10 of 0.25 or of 0.75 (quadratures, where an ellipsoidal variable is brightest). Coverages: W0 20%, W1 20%, Q 40%.

## Predictions (frozen)

- A1 (primary; confidence 70%): at least 50% of FAINT points fall in W0, and the one-sided binomial P(k >= k_obs | n, p = 0.20) < 1e-3.
- A2 (null control; 75%): at most 15% of FAINT points fall in Q (chance expectation 40%). An ellipsoidal variable at Gaia's period should rarely be faint at quadrature.
- A3 (eclipse beyond ellipsoidal; 60%): the number of FAINT points in W0 is at least twice the number in W1. Under pure ellipsoidal variation the two conjunctions would be equally faint; the Gaia epochs put an extra ~0.05 mag at conj0.
- A4 (period check; 65%): the mean phase of the W0 FAINT points lies within +/-0.04 of 0.000 (the carried-back Gaia ephemeris is right to within its stated error).

Verdicts: A1 and A2 PASS -> "ASAS-3 confirms Gaia-phased dimming of BD+12 3880 at Gaia's conj0 back to 2000-2009" (A3 then decides whether the dimming is eclipse-like or purely ellipsoidal; neither outcome is claimed as a new eclipsing binary until TESS runs). A1 FAIL with n_faint >= 15 -> the test FAILS and we say so (the Gaia variability does not persist at Gaia's period, or the period is wrong; we will not search for another period in this data). n_faint < 15 -> INCONCLUSIVE regardless. Nothing is fitted to ASAS-3; thresholds and windows are not changed after the data are seen.
