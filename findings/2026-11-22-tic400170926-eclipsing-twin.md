## 1. Result

**TIC 400170926 (TYC 2423-351-1, 2MASS J06033997+3220159, Gaia DR3 3450611154067089408; RA 06h03m40s,
Dec +32°20′ in Auriga; G 9.33, 128 pc) is a detached eclipsing binary of two Sun-like stars. Its eclipses repeat every
2.94788 d, exactly half of Gaia DR3's double-lined orbit period (5.895935 ± 0.000182 d).** Gaia's
transit list (`vari_planetary_transit`) and classifier (`vari_classifier_result`, class EP, score
1.0) carry it as a planetary-transit candidate at 0.88478 d. We found it in no eclipsing-binary
catalogue, no TOI list and no paper.

## 3. Gaia's orbit gives the masses

Gaia DR3 `nss_two_body_orbit`, SB2C (circular, double-lined), significance 180.3,
goodness_of_fit 1.09: K1 = 72.62 ± 0.40 km/s, K2 = 78.94 ± 1.01 km/s (values in
`data/vpt-nss-dr3/vpt_nss_dr3.csv`). From the standard relations
(M sin³i = 1.0361e-7 (K1+K2)² K P, a sin i = 0.019758 (K1+K2) P; K in km/s, P in days):

- M1 sin³i = **1.108 ± 0.030 M_sun**, M2 sin³i = **1.019 ± 0.017 M_sun** (Gaia's formal K errors
  only, propagated without correlations), a sin i = 17.66 R_sun.
- Eclipses need i close to 90°. For i between 82° and 90°, sin³i is 0.971-1.000, so
  **M1 = 1.11-1.14 M_sun and M2 = 1.02-1.05 M_sun**. Gaia's own isochrone masses
  (`binary_masses`, SB2+M1: 1.054 and 0.970 M_sun) are about 5-8% lower.
- **Geometric consistency check (illustrative, not a fit):** a 3.17 h eclipse on a 5.896 d orbit
  with a ≈ 17.7 R_sun means a projected chord half-length of π a T / P ≈ 1.25 R_sun. If the two
  stars' radii add up to about 2.0 R_sun (the TIC's 1.41 R_sun single-star radius split between
  two similar stars), the projected separation at mid-eclipse is ≈ 1.55 R_sun, so i ≈ 85° and the
  eclipse is partial. A uniform-disc overlap for two 1.0 R_sun stars at that separation hides about
  6% of the total light, close to the observed 5.3-6.1% folded depths. A real light-curve fit with
  limb darkening is still needed.

So TIC 400170926 is a bright (G 9.33, T 8.93) double-lined eclipsing binary of two stars of about
1.1 and 1.0 solar masses on a 5.9-day circular orbit. Gaia already gives the minimum masses to
about 2-3%; an eclipse fit gives the inclination and both radii. Double-lined eclipsing binaries
with masses and radii known to about 2% are the standard test of stellar models (Torres,
Andersen & Giménez 2010; DEBCat, Southworth 2015), and bright, uncatalogued ones are rare.

## 4. Gaia's transit period

Gaia's planet-transit period (0.8847835 d) is not a simple fraction of the orbit (5.895935 /
0.8847835 = 6.664). But **three eclipse intervals almost equal ten Gaia periods**: 2.9478808 /
0.8847835 = 3.33175 against 10/3 = 3.33333 (0.047% apart). We do not yet know how Gaia's sparse
sampling picked this period; the queued epoch test (gaia-vpt-epochs-v1) will show whether Gaia's
in-transit epochs fall on real eclipses. Gaia's 20.6 mmag (about 1.9%) transit depth is about a
third of the real eclipse depth.

## 6. A pilot: how often do Gaia's edge-on double-lined orbits eclipse in TESS?

Rule (fixed before looking at TESS): Gaia DR3 SB2 solutions with `binary_masses` method SB2+M1,
P < 8 d, and M1 sin³i between 0.95 and 1.10 times Gaia's m1 (i.e. sin³i ≈ 1). That keeps **184**
systems, 145 of them fainter than G = 9.0. The 20 brightest of those 145 (G 9.02-9.39) were
mapped to TIC (ExoFOP) and their SPOC TCE lists read from exo.MAST. Data:
`data/sb2-edgeon-pilot/sb2_edgeon_pilot.csv`.

- **7 of 20 have SPOC TCEs, and all 7 TCE periods are half of Gaia's orbit period** to within
  0.03% (six within 0.007%): twin eclipses, as expected for SB2s with K1 ≈ K2.
- 13 have no SPOC TCE. That does not mean they do not eclipse: SPOC's 2-min search covers only
  stars that got 2-min data, and eclipses can be too shallow or grazing.
- **6 of the 7 are already known:** V1010 Cas, ASAS J085256+1449.0, NV TrA and VV Crt (VSX),
  TOI-631 (retired by TFOP as an eclipsing binary; CORALIE saw an equal-luminosity SB2), and HIP
  34208 (in the TESS EB catalogue, P 2.4374384 d, but not in VSX). **TIC 400170926 is the only one
  we found in no catalogue.**
- The screen works as a finder of eclipsing twins with Gaia masses. At G < 9 it returns famous
  eclipsing binaries (e.g. Gaia DR3 66729880383767168, P 2.4612 d, at the position of the Pleiades
  eclipsing binary HD 23642). The bright end is mostly known, so new systems, if any, will be in the
  fainter 125 not yet checked and among the 13 without TCEs (FFI light curves).
- Prior work: Rowan et al. (2023, MNRAS, doi:10.1093/mnras/stad1560; arXiv:2212.02509) matched Gaia DR3 SB2 orbits
  with ASAS-SN eclipsing binaries to measure masses and radii; ASAS-SN saturates near V ≈ 10-11,
  so bright SB2s such as TIC 400170926 fall outside it. Gaia DR3 itself combines its eclipsing and
  SB2 solutions for 53 stars (`binary_masses` method Eclipsing+SB2).

## Limits

Exploratory. The eclipse parameters are SPOC's planet-model fits to a binary (depth and period are
reliable; impact parameter, a/R* and radius ratio are not physical for two stars). The mass
ranges assume Gaia's formal K errors; Gaia DR3 SB2 velocities of blended lines can be biased, and
ground-based spectra are needed for benchmark-grade masses. SIMBAD and the TESS Ten Thousand
catalogue could not be checked. All values were transcribed from archive responses whose URLs are
given above; anyone can re-run them.
