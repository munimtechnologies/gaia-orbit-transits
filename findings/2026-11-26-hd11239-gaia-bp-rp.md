# HD 11239: the Gaia eclipse dips are in all three photometers and colour-neutral

Text CC BY 4.0; code Apache-2.0. Written 2026-11-26 ~07:00 UTC. Code: hd11239_gaia_bprp.py 0.1.0
(test: test_hd11239_gaia_bprp.py, which asserts every number below). Data:

**This is a vetting step, not a pre-registered test.** HD 11239 was selected by the blind G-band
rule g8 (findings/2026-11-26-g8-dr3-blind-results.md) because it has faint G epochs at Gaia's
conjunctions, so the G-band statistics below are not independent evidence. What is new here is
(1) BP and RP, which rule g8 never looked at, (2) a check for neighbours, and (3) the shape of the
dips against phase. The pre-registered TESS test (findings/2026-11-26-hd11239-tess-prereg.md,
job hd11239-tess-v1) is still queued and remains the deciding test.

## Results
Baselines: medians of the good epochs outside both windows: G 9.5827 (80 epochs), BP 9.87315 (82),
RP 9.1222 (81). Robust scatter of G (1.4826 x MAD) about 0.004 mag.

Five transits fall inside a window. **All five are fainter than the baseline in all three bands
(15 of 15):**

| 7 | 1686.212 | -0.0028 (conj. 0) | +0.0856 | +0.0870 | +0.0847 |
| 16 | 1715.906 | +0.0098 (conj. 1) | +0.0536 | +0.0538 | +0.0578 |
| 67 | 2433.715 | +0.0159 (conj. 1) | +0.0400 | +0.0357 | +0.0360 |

| 6 | 1686.138 | -0.0265 (conj. 0) | +0.0083 | +0.0075 | +0.0100 |

1. **The three deep dips appear in BP and RP with the same depth.** BP/RP depth ratios 1.027, 0.930
   and 0.990 (mean 0.982). BP and RP are separate photometers with their own CCD windows, so an
   artefact in the G window would not repeat there. Equal depths in blue and red are what an
   eclipse between two nearly identical F stars gives; a blended eclipsing star of a different
   colour would dim one band more.
2. **Gaia's own G-only outlier behaves differently.** Transit 76 (phase +0.072, outside both
   windows) is +0.0242 mag faint in G and flagged by Gaia as rejected; in BP and RP it is only
   +0.0061 and +0.0055, within the scatter. The in-window dips are not of that kind.
3. **Depth falls with distance from conjunction**, across both conjunctions and five different
   orbits: 0.086 at 0.003, 0.054 at 0.010, 0.040 at 0.016, 0.011 at 0.024, 0.008 at 0.027.
   No good epoch outside the windows is fainter than +0.0099 mag in G (transit 65), and the
   five epochs at 0.041-0.054 from a conjunction (transits 69, 15, 62, 33, 8) are all within the
   scatter. So the eclipses last at most about +-0.03 of an orbit (about +-2.2 hours).
4. **Plausible geometry.** With Gaia's minimum masses (1.09 and 1.05 Msun) and P = 3.121 d the
   stars are about 11.6 solar radii apart. Two F stars of 1.1-1.5 solar radii would give a central
   eclipse of up to +-0.03-0.04 of an orbit; a depth of only about 0.09 mag at both conjunctions
   means partial (grazing) eclipses of near-equal stars, so the inclination is a few degrees below
   90 and the true masses a few per cent above Gaia's minimum masses. This is an order-of-magnitude
   consistency check, not a fit.
5. For reference (not independent, since rule g8 chose the star on G): the three faintest of the
   85 good G epochs are all inside the windows (chance 10 / 98,770 = 1.0e-4 if the 5 in-window
   epochs were random draws).

## What this means
Every check available from Gaia's own data passes: the dips are at the conjunctions of Gaia's
own double-lined orbit, at both of them, in three separate photometers, with equal depths in blue
and red, with a depth-versus-phase profile like an eclipse, and with no Gaia source within one
TESS pixel. HD 11239 is a strong candidate double-lined eclipsing binary of two F8 stars. We still
call it a candidate until the pre-registered TESS test (hd11239-tess-v1) is graded.

## Limits
- Five epochs. Gaia samples each eclipse once, so these are single-epoch depths, not eclipse depths.
- BP and RP are measured during the same transit as G, so they guard against detector artefacts,
  not against a real but unrelated brightness change at those times.
- The CSV values are rounded transcriptions (0.001 d, 0.0001 mag); rounding changes no result.
- The geometry in point 4 uses assumed radii; no light-curve model was fitted.
- Made with AI agents; no human expert has checked it.
