## Why
HD 11239 (Gaia DR3 4961129715887496320) is the one unlisted flag of the blind Gaia DR3 eclipse
search. All evidence so far is Gaia's own (5 epochs). ASAS-3 (Pojmanski; star 014936-3944.5,
2000-2009, V band) is a fully independent instrument and epoch range.

## Data
http://www.astrouw.edu.pl/cgi-asas/asas_cgi_get_data?014936-3944.5,asas3 (fetched 26 Nov 2026).
All datasets; grade A and B rows only; aperture MAG_3 (ASAS's recommended aperture for V 9-10).
Time column HJD - 2450000.

## Ephemeris (from Gaia DR3 alone, nothing fitted to ASAS)
Gaia time = BJD - 2455197.5, so t_gaia = HJD' - 5197.5 (HJD vs BJD_TCB differ by < 0.002 d).
Conjunction 0 at HJD' 7389.29453; P = 3.1208263847 +- 0.0000391 d; conjunction 1 at phase
+0.50223. Propagated back ~1,100-1,780 orbits to the ASAS epochs, the period error alone gives a
1-sigma phase uncertainty of about 0.014-0.022, so the window is widened to |dphase| <= 0.06
(fraction of the orbit covered by both windows: 0.24).

## Definitions
- Baseline: median MAG_3 of all grade A+B rows. Faint point: MAG_3 >= baseline + 0.07 mag.
- dphase: signed phase from the nearer of the two Gaia conjunctions.

## Tests
- A1: at least 5 faint points, and at least 60% of them with |dphase| <= 0.06 (chance 24%).
- A2: in-window faint points occur near BOTH conjunctions and on at least 3 different nights.
Both pass -> ASAS-3 independently confirms eclipses at Gaia's conjunctions. Either fails -> say so;
the queued TESS test (hd11239-tess-v1) still decides.
