# Pre-registration: an independent Gaia DR4 test of TYC 6340-23-1's mass

Written 2026-11-20 (about 07:20 UTC), **before Gaia DR4 exists** (release 2 December 2026, 12:00
CET). By MV2 of Munim, Inc.; made with AI agents working in his name, not checked by a human
expert. Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0. Code: `tyc_k_dr4.py` 0.1.0 with tests `test_tyc_k_dr4.py`.

## The question

TYC 6340-23-1 (TIC 71945261, Gaia DR3 6853008555751131648; V = 10.7, 110.9 pc) is a new 2.3287-day
eclipsing system that we found in Gaia DR3's spectroscopic-orbit catalogue
(`findings/2026-11-19-tic71945261-fit-results.md`). Its companion is 0.224 R_sun across. Gaia
DR3's single-lined orbit (K = 16.16 ± 0.48 km/s) makes it 0.097 M_sun, which is 1.9 times the
radius a normal star of that mass should have (label B_OVERSIZED_COMPANION). Our pre-registered
youth check found it moves like a field star, not a member of any known young group
(`findings/2026-11-20-tic71945261-followup-results.md`). The biggest open question is whether
Gaia's K is right. A normal-size companion (about 0.21 M_sun) would need **K ≈ 32.4 km/s**,
twice Gaia's value. No archival survey (RAVE, GALAH, APOGEE) observed the star, and Gaia's own
15 epochs agree with its orbit, but the orbit was fitted to those same epochs.

Gaia DR3 used data up to **28 May 2017** (JD 2457901.5). Gaia DR4 covers 66 months, to January
2020, and is expected to publish per-transit (epoch) radial velocities. **Epochs after
28 May 2017 were not used by the DR3 orbit**, so they test K independently.

## Rule (fixed now)

1. Get the target's DR4 epoch radial velocities through Gaia DataLink with dr4_sb1_reality's
   pinned plumbing (`link_types`, `choose_rv_type`, `fetch_epoch_rows`; release v0.28.0,
   commit 25020a8). If the epoch-RV retrieval type is not uniquely identified (exit 6), a dated
   amendment names it **before any velocity is read**, and the job is re-run.
2. Count an epoch if it has a finite RV and a positive error, its `outlier_status` is not set and
   `emission_lines` is not set (dr4_sb1_reality amendment 1, rule 3). Times are converted to
   Julian date (full JD, MJD or Gaia time, days since 2010-01-01).
3. **Independent epochs** are counted epochs with JD > 2457901.5.
4. Fit v = γ + a cos(2πt/P) + b sin(2πt/P) to the independent epochs, weighted by 1/σ², at
   Gaia DR3's period P = 2.3289298 d with free phase. K = √(a² + b²). σ_K is taken from the
   covariance and multiplied by √χ²_red when χ²_red > 1 (never shrunk).
5. Label:
   - **UNTESTABLE**: fewer than 6 independent epochs, no epoch RVs for this source, or no fit.
   - **NO_ORBIT_SIGNAL**: K/σ_K < 3.
   - Otherwise let G = |K − 16.16| ≤ 3·√(σ_K² + 0.48²) and H = |K − 32.4| ≤ 3·σ_K.
     **GAIA_K_CONFIRMED** if G and not H; **HEAVY_K** if H and not G; **UNDECIDED** if both;
     **OTHER_K** if neither.
6. Reported, not graded: the fit at the TESS period (2.3287 d); the fit to all counted epochs
   (not independent); the phase of the fitted conjunction relative to the TESS primary eclipse
   (BJD 2460803.0299; Gaia's period error makes this uncertain by about ±0.06 in phase); the
   target's DR4 orbit and solution type from release-day job (a1) (an SB2 solution would be
   evidence for a blend).

## What each outcome means (fixed now)

- **GAIA_K_CONFIRMED:** the mass 0.097 M_sun is supported by data independent of the DR3 orbit.
  The companion stays B_OVERSIZED_COMPANION. What remains open is youth outside known groups
  (lithium) and a confirmation of the radius from a second TESS sector or an independent fit. Our
  eclipse pipeline failed its holdout check (`findings/2026-11-20-eblm-holdout-results.md`),
  so the radius still needs that confirmation. The article
  (munimtech.com/research/tyc-6340-23-1-oversized-companion) gets an update.
- **HEAVY_K:** Gaia DR3's K for this star is wrong by a factor of about two, and the companion is
  a normal M dwarf of about 0.21 M_sun. The B_OVERSIZED_COMPANION label is **withdrawn** in
  STATUS, the findings and the article. The system then becomes a documented case of a DR3 orbit
  that passes the eclipse test with the wrong amplitude.
- **OTHER_K / NO_ORBIT_SIGNAL:** neither hypothesis fits. A blend or a hierarchical triple
  becomes the leading explanation. The label is suspended until spectra exist.
- **UNDECIDED / UNTESTABLE:** nothing changes.

## Predictions (written now)

GAIA_K_CONFIRMED 60%, HEAVY_K 10%, OTHER_K 5%, NO_ORBIT_SIGNAL 2%, UNDECIDED 3%,
UNTESTABLE 20%. The main reason for UNTESTABLE: DR4 may not publish usable epoch RVs for this
source, or DataLink may differ from what the pinned plumbing expects. The reason for favouring
GAIA_K_CONFIRMED: Gaia's DR3 RV range (`rv_amplitude_robust` 32.5 km/s) matches 2K = 32.3 km/s,
not the 64.8 km/s the heavy hypothesis needs. That is the same data, so it is a reason for the
prediction, not evidence for the result.

## Limits

One star, one test. A right K does not exclude a blend that dilutes the eclipses: dilution would
make the true radius ratio larger, not smaller. A right K also does not exclude youth outside
known groups. An orbit with eccentricity is fitted as circular (DR3 gives e = 0.04).
Gaia's radial velocities for a G = 10.5 star have per-epoch errors of a few km/s, so about
10-15 independent epochs give σ_K ≈ 1-1.5 km/s. That is enough to separate 16 from 32 km/s, but
not to measure small differences.
