## Grading

- **A1 (>= 50% of faint points in W0, binomial P < 1e-3): PASS, at the bound.** 53 of 106 = 50.0% (by nights 50 of 87 = 57%). Chance expectation 21.2 +/- 4.1; the one-sided binomial tail for k >= 53 is below 1e-10.
- **A2 (<= 15% of faint points in Q): PASS.** 8 of 106 = 7.5% against 40% coverage (42 expected by chance). The star is almost never faint at quadrature.
- **A3 (W0 count >= 2 x W1 count): FAIL.** 53 vs 43 (ratio 1.23); by nights 50 vs 27 (ratio 1.85). The two conjunctions collect faint points almost equally.
- **A4 (mean phase of W0 faint points within +/-0.04 of 0): PASS.** Mean +0.011 (53 points; 33 at positive phase summing to +1.516, 20 at negative phase summing to -0.914). The Gaia DR3 ephemeris carried back 1,330-2,440 orbits is right to about 0.01 of an orbit (0.02 d).

Pre-registered verdict (A1 and A2 PASS, A3 FAIL): **ASAS-3 confirms that Gaia's orbital ephemeris phases BD+12 3880's dimming from 2002-2009: 96 of 106 faint points (91%) lie within 0.10 of an orbit of one of Gaia's two conjunctions, against 40% coverage. ASAS-3 sees the ellipsoidal double wave at both conjunctions; it does not show the 2:1 excess at conj0 that the extra eclipse seen in Gaia's seven faint epochs would give.** The eclipse at conj0 therefore still rests on Gaia's epochs alone until the queued TESS test (sb1-tier2-tess-v1, predictions L1-L3) runs. BD+12 3880 is not claimed as an eclipsing binary.

## Exploratory, not pre-registered

The depth distribution differs between the two windows. Excluding the bad night, points at least 0.15 mag below the median (MAG_0 >= 9.20) number 13 in W0 (HJD 2731.90, 2819.74, 2833.75, 2835.64, 2855.63, 2859.68, 2861.71, 2885.55, 2927.54, 4330.56, 4583.88, 4701.65, 4705.67), 3 in W1 (2838.68, 2854.57, 2932.52) and 1 in Q (3903.82). An eclipse at conj0 deeper than the ellipsoidal minimum would produce exactly this kind of asymmetry in the deepest points, but ASAS-3 outliers at V 9 next to a saturated V 5.5 star are common, the threshold was chosen after seeing the data, and the pre-registered A3 test failed. We record it as a hint for the TESS grading, nothing more. MAG_1 (check aperture) is systematically 0.03-0.15 mag brighter than MAG_0 for the faintest points, as expected if the deepest MAG_0 values are partly noise or if the larger aperture admits light from V1208 Aql.

## What this adds

VSX lists BD+12 3880 only as a Gaia DR3 "VAR" with period 0.99786 d (half the Gaia orbital period), with no connection to an orbit. This note shows, from an independent instrument and decade, that the dimming is locked to the Gaia DR3 spectroscopic orbit: minima at both conjunctions, none at quadrature, ephemeris good to 0.01 of an orbit over 2,400 orbits. It does not show an eclipse.
