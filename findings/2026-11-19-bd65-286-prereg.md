before this was written). Taken at face value, the depth gives R₂/R₁ ≈ 0.063 and
R₂ ≈ 0.137 R☉ with the FLAME radius, about 10 % above a main-sequence 0.1 M☉ star before any
limb-darkening correction.

The star is **not in the rate sample** of the transit-rate test: its injection sensitivity is
0.0. The injected box is 0.8 × the expected depth (0.26 %), and the folded light curve keeps a
±0.1 % ellipsoidal residual, so injections likely land below the S/N ≥ 7 rule while the real
0.40 % eclipse reaches S/N 9–10. It therefore does not change ρ either way.

So the honest claim is narrow: **SPOC saw the dip, but nobody has published BD+65 286 as an
eclipsing system, and nobody has tied the dip to Gaia's orbit, which gives the companion a
mass.** With the eclipse, sin i ≈ 1, so Gaia's 0.098 M☉ becomes close to the true mass.

## Why it is worth a fit
Transiting stars near 0.1 M☉ with a measured mass are rare, and they test models of fully
convective stars (the "M-dwarf radius problem"). At V ≈ 9.7, BD+65 286 would be among the
brightest such systems in the northern sky. The ellipsoidal signal is also strong: for 0.098
M☉ at a/R₁ ≈ 3.15 our rough estimate is ~2,000–3,000 ppm. The fit therefore checks Gaia's
mass directly from the light curve, independently of the eclipse.

## What the job does
Job `gaia-bd65286-v1` runs the **unchanged** `eblm_single.py` 0.1.0 (fetched at commit
04c9387, release v0.23.0), which runs the unchanged `eblm_fit` 0.1.0 (commit 10b5aeb, the code
that fit 167 Gaia EBLMs) on up to 8 TESS sectors. The fit gives k, b, a/R₁, R₁ from the
light-curve density (vs FLAME: `r1_ratio`), the ellipsoidal amplitude vs the Gaia-mass
prediction (`ell_ratio_fit`), the occultation depth and companion temperature, and odd/even
depths. BLS refines the period within ±0.1 % of Gaia's. Outcome labels are the ones fixed in
`findings/2026-11-18-tic71945261-prereg.md` (A, A2, B, B0, C, D, E, G, UNTESTED).

Two more systems run in the same job:
- **TOI-7247.01** (Gaia DR3 1802445358630815104, TIC 51318828), the substellar-size hit of
  chunk 5. TFOP lists it as a false positive, citing an uploaded Gaia DR3 NSS orbit
  (K = 9.95 km/s, i.e. a ~0.09 M☉ companion). A 2026 RAVEN upload lists it as a
  high-probability planet. The fit tests whether the eclipse fits a ~0.09 M☉ star.
- **TOI-1251.01** (Gaia DR3 2160710158704303360), the same comparison system as in the
  TIC 71945261 job.

## Predictions, written now
- **BD+65 286: C (EBLM_CONSISTENT)**, with inflation `infl` between −0.10 and +0.25,
  `ell_ratio_fit` between 0.5 and 2 (Gaia's mass confirmed by the ellipsoidal signal), and
  `r1_ratio` between 0.8 and 1.25. Not grazing (we expect b ≈ 0.6–0.7, as SPOC found).
  Any other label is reported as it comes out.
- **TOI-7247.01: C or B.** The face-value depth (1.13 % on R₁ = 1.32 R☉) gives R₂ ≈ 0.14 R☉,
  about 25 % above a main-sequence 0.09 M☉ star, which is close to the B line (+30 %). Its
  ellipsoidal signal is too small (~80 ppm) to test the mass. Either C or B means the
  transiting body is a star-sized companion of the Gaia orbit, which supports the TFOP false
  positive over the RAVEN planet call **only if** the Gaia orbit is real. That is exactly the
  question the parent study asks, so we will say so rather than adjudicate.
- **TOI-1251.01: C**, as in the earlier pre-registration. If it comes out otherwise, the other
  two labels are not interpreted.
