## Question

Gaia DR3 published 181,327 single-lined spectroscopic orbits (SB1). For a binary with a short
period, Gaia's radial velocities give the period and both conjunction times, so a few faint
epochs of Gaia's own photometry at the predicted conjunctions, in two different eclipses, are
enough to show that it eclipses. Gaia's eclipsing-binary classifier (Mowlavi et al. 2023) had to
find the period from the sparse photometry alone. **How many Gaia DR3 single-lined binaries with
periods of 0.4-15 days eclipse in Gaia's own photometry although Gaia does not call them
eclipsing?** The wow, if it works: a batch of new eclipsing binaries, found by Gaia alone, whose
radial-velocity orbit is already known, including faint and crowded stars where TESS's 21-arcsecond
pixels fail, and, among the shallowest, low-mass companions (M dwarfs, brown-dwarf-mass objects) to
Sun-like stars.

Prior work searched (25 Nov 2026, ~20:45 UTC): web search "Gaia DR3 single-lined spectroscopic
binaries SB1 epoch photometry eclipses at conjunction search"; Rowan et al. 2023
(arXiv:2207.05086, high-mass-function SB1s vetted with ASAS-SN and TESS, 31 eclipsing); Lin et al.
arXiv:2310.07936 (2023, SB1 x TOIs/TESS EBs, three transiting low-mass companions; it notes that
Gaia's sparse photometry is insensitive to all but the shortest periods); arXiv:2310.19307 (2023,
EL CVn stars from Gaia's EB table x SB1); arXiv:2608.06453 (2026, spectroscopic
follow-up of Gaia compact-object candidates); our own eblm_screen (Gaia SB1 x Gaia's own EB table,
i.e. only stars Gaia already calls eclipsing); Eclipses by Appointment (GitHub, folds TESS at Gaia
periods; newest commit 64fbde8 of 25 Nov 19:44 UTC, whose review notes say Gaia contributes the
orbits, not the photometry); Chopard-Vilhem 2026 (Zenodo, SB1 x TESS, P 13-200 d). None runs a
conjunction test on Gaia's own epoch photometry for all short-period DR3 SB1 orbits.

## Sample (fixed)

All rows of AIP `gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit` x `gaiadr3.gaia_source` with
`nss_solution_type LIKE 'SB1%'`, `0.4 <= period <= 15.0` d and `has_epoch_photometry = 'True'`.
Counted on 25 Nov before the run: **7,978 stars (7,973 SB1, 5 SB1C)**. Without the epoch
condition, 2,157 SB1 orbits in this period range are in Gaia's `vari_eclipsing_binary` table.
The job stops (exit 2) if the sample query and the count disagree; it reports the count beside
the pre-registered 7,978.

## Method (fixed)

- Epochs: `gaiadr3.epoch_photometry` G transit times and magnitudes from the AIP mirror, 25 stars
  per query; a failed chunk is retried star by star; if more than 1% of stars stay missing the job
  stops (exit 2), otherwise the missing stars are listed and left out.
- An epoch is dropped if `variability_flag_g_reject` is True or its magnitude is not finite.
- Rule: `sb2_ecl_dr4.classify` 0.1.0 unchanged: 2-harmonic Fourier model in orbital phase fitted
  away from the conjunctions (so ellipsoidal and reflection light is modelled), dip = residual >=
  max(0.025 mag, 4 sd), ECL_GAIA = >= 2 in-window dips (|dphase| <= 0.035) from >= 2 different
  eclipses and in-window >= 2 x out-of-window dips; ECL_GAIA_SHALLOW; FEW_EPOCHS below 20 usable
  epochs. Orbit time = 2191.5 + t_periastron. Conjunctions at true anomaly 90 deg - omega
  (conjunction 0: the visible star is behind its companion) and 270 deg - omega; 0.25/0.75 for
  circular orbits. Null: both conjunctions moved by 0.25 in phase (quadrature).
- "Gaia-known eclipsing" = in `vari_eclipsing_binary`, or any NSS solution of the source whose
  type contains "clipsing", or `vari_classifier_result.best_class_name = 'ECL'`.
- Flagged = ECL_GAIA or ECL_GAIA_SHALLOW. "New candidate" = strict ECL_GAIA and not Gaia-known.

## Predictions (P1-P4 and P6 graded automatically by the job, P5 by hand)

| # | Prediction | Threshold | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Share of Gaia-known eclipsing stars flagged (lower than the double-lined 0.40, because Gaia's EB table also holds ellipsoidal variables and single-lined eclipses are often shallow) | >= 0.25 | 55% |
| P2 | Null (quadrature) flags / classified stars | <= 0.02 | 70% |
| P3 | Median abs(dphase) of the in-window dips of ECL_GAIA stars | <= 0.015 | 75% |
| P4 | New candidates (ECL_GAIA, not Gaia-known) | >= 30 | 55% |
| P5 | Of the 10 deepest new candidates (all if fewer), hand-vetted against VSX, SIMBAD, Prsa 2022, IJspeert 2024, TESS Ten Thousand, Eclipses by Appointment (latest commit) and a web search of the Gaia id, at least half are listed in none | >= 50% | 50% |
| P6 | Among ECL_GAIA stars, the deepest in-window dip lies at conjunction 0 (the visible, brighter star eclipsed by its fainter companion), with at least 20 such stars | share >= 0.65 | 75% |

P6 is a physics check that the double-lined run cannot make: in a single-lined binary the star
whose lines Gaia measured dominates the light, so its eclipse should be the deeper one, and it
happens when that star is farthest from us (true anomaly 90 deg - omega in the radial-velocity
convention RV = K [cos(nu + omega) + e cos omega]). Our eblm_screen found the same for 18 of Gaia's
EclipsingSpectro systems; here it is tested blind, on dips the rule found by itself.

Also reported (not graded): the purity estimate = (null flags among not-Gaia-known stars) / (new
candidates); the same P6 share among new candidates only; the Gaia variability class of the new
candidates (a pulsator class such as RR, CEP or DSCT|GDOR|SXPHE is a warning sign: some Gaia SB1
solutions with short periods are pulsations, not orbits).

## What counts as success and what does not

Success = P2 and P3 true (the flags are tied to Gaia's conjunctions, not noise) and P4 true; P6
true makes the eclipse interpretation much stronger. A star is only called a new eclipsing binary
after P5's vetting finds it in no catalogue, and only with its epochs and fold published. Stars
whose Gaia class is a pulsator are reported but not called eclipsing without other evidence. If P2
or P3 fails, the candidates are not reported as eclipsing. If the AIP mirror fails or returns a
different sample size, the job is re-filed unchanged (version bumped only after a code failure),
and nothing here changes.
