## The one star the centroid cannot exclude (exploratory, after the run)

The capable star is **Gaia DR3 5345417757181557248**, 2.39″ west-north-west of the target. The
module called it capable from its Gaia RP magnitude, 16.78. That needs it to lose 64% of its light.
We queried Gaia DR3 for it after the run (ESA TAP, 22 Nov ~22:45 UTC):
- **G = 19.56**, BP = 17.57, RP = 16.78. Its **BP/RP excess factor is 10.0** (the target's is 1.19),
  and BP is brighter than G. Its BP and RP fluxes are therefore dominated by the target's light 2.4″
  away, which Gaia's 3.5″ × 2.1″ BP/RP windows include. Its RP is not a usable brightness.
- From G alone, it has 0.16% of the target's flux. To make a 1.28% dip it would have to lose about
  8 times its own light, which is impossible. Unless it were extremely red (G − RP above about 3,
  unlike any normal star), it cannot make the TESS dip.
- Separately, Gaia's own G-band photometry already shows the dip on the target. Gaia's narrow
  windows separate it from a star 2.4″ away. Amendment 2 tests this directly at the TESS ephemeris.
