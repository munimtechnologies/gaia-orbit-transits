# TYC 7628-1267-1: does ASAS-3 (2000-2009) show the TESS eclipses, and on which period? (pre-registration)

MV2, Munim, Inc. (mv2@munimtech.com). Written 2026-11-28 ~04:20 UTC, BEFORE any ASAS-3 data for this
star were fetched. Made with AI agents working in MV2's name; no human expert has checked it. Text CC BY 4.0.

## Why
TYC 7628-1267-1 (TIC 148478890, Gaia DR3 5576469852185715200, G 10.86) is a Gaia DR3 double-lined
binary (I/357/tbosb2: P = 7.25085 +/- 0.00046 d, e = 0.056, omega = 283 deg, M1 sin^3 i 1.42, M2 sin^3 i
1.35 Msun). Our scan sb2-ffiscan-t2-v1 found a 4.1% eclipse in TESS (6 sectors, QLP/SPOC) with
P_TESS = 7.248555 d, T0 = 2590.83739 BTJD, duration 0.290 d, and a 1.9% secondary near phase 0.465.
Two things are unresolved: P_TESS is 4.9 sigma from Gaia's period, and the neighbour TYC 7628-1335-1
(G 11.46, 31") shares the TESS pixels (pixel test tier2-pixvet-v1 is queued). ASAS-3, 15" pixels,
also blends the two stars, so it cannot say WHICH star eclipses. It can say whether the eclipse has
kept P_TESS for ~20 years (about 700 orbits back from T0), which separates the two periods: the
ephemerides drift apart by 700 x 0.0023 d = 1.6 d = 0.22 of an orbit at the ASAS-3 epochs.

## Data and rule (frozen)
- ASAS-3 V light curve, www.astrouw.edu.pl/cgi-asas/asas_cgi_get_data?065034-3809.5,asas3 (if that id
  does not resolve, the nearest ASAS-3 source within 30" of 06 50 34.06 -38 09 28.1). Grades A and B
  only; the aperture column used is the one with the smallest median error (state which).
- BTJD = HJD - 2457000 (ASAS gives HJD - 2450000; so BTJD = HJD' - 7000).
- Faint point: V >= median V + 0.05 mag.
- Ephemeris T (TESS): T0 = 2590.83739 BTJD, P = 7.248555 d. Ephemeris G (Gaia period): same T0,
  P = 7.25085 d.
- Window: phase within +/-0.035 of the primary (0.035 = half the 0.040 duration in phase plus 0.015
  for period-error drift).
- Null window: phase within +/-0.035 of 0.25 and of 0.75 (counted separately, each its own null).

## Predictions (probabilities written now)
- A1 (65%): under ephemeris T, at least 5 faint points on at least 4 different nights fall in the primary
  window, and the binomial chance of that many or more, with p0 = fraction of ALL good points in the
  window, is < 1e-3.
- A2 (75%, conditional on A1): under ephemeris G, the primary-window faint count is at most half of the
  count under T (the Gaia period does not fit the ASAS-3 timing).
- A3 (85%): neither null window passes the A1 rule.
- Outcomes. A1 + A2 + A3: the eclipse has held P_TESS since 2000-2009; Gaia's SB2 period is then either
  ~5 sigma off or belongs to a different star than the eclipse (to be decided by the pixel test).
  A1 fails: ASAS-3 is too noisy for a 4% eclipse at V~11, reported as inconclusive (not as evidence
  against). No claim of a new eclipsing binary is made from this test alone.
