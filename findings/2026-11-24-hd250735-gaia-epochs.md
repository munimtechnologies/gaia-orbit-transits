# HD 250735: Gaia's own photometry caught two of its eclipses (exploratory)

Text and data CC BY 4.0. Written 2026-11-24 (~06:00 UTC).

Status: **exploratory, not a registered grade.** Test E4 of `findings/2026-11-23-sb2-new-ebs-prereg.md`
is graded only by job sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1, which is still waiting for compute. That job fetches
Gaia epochs through ESA DataLink. ESA's archive closes on 25 Nov 12:00 CET until Gaia DR4 (2 Dec), so
if the job runs after that, its E4 for HD 250735 becomes UNTESTABLE as registered. To keep the data,
we saved HD 250735's Gaia DR3 G-band epochs now and report what they show by hand. We have now seen

## Data

  3450611154067089408 (HD 250735, TIC 400170926), from

  `variability_flag_g_reject` true and is 0.013 mag *brighter* than the median.
- Times: Gaia gives barycentric JD (TCB) − 2455197.5. BTJD = that − 1802.5 (TCB − TDB is about 19 s
  here and is ignored).
- The values were transcribed from the archive's CSV by a text tool. Anyone can check them by re-running
  the URL above until 25 Nov 12:00 CET, or from ESA's bulk epoch-photometry files after that.
- The same request for TYC 3616-2395-1 (Gaia DR3 1980920834829359488) returned an empty response,

## What the epochs show

Applying the E4 rule as registered: median 9.33385 mag, MAD 0.00298 mag, faint threshold
max(5 MAD, 0.02) = 0.020 mag. **Exactly two epochs are faint:**

| Gaia time | BTJD | G (mag) | below median | eclipse cycle n (P/2 grid) | offset from predicted mid-eclipse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1934.82440 | 132.3244 | 9.37099 | 0.0371 | −795 | **−0.0609 d** |
| 2592.21858 | 789.7186 | 9.35862 | 0.0248 | −572 | **−0.0442 d** |

The next-faintest epoch (time 2258.639, 0.0188 mag below the median) is under the threshold. It falls
0.51 d from the nearest predicted eclipse.

- Ephemeris: SPOC TCE 1, sectors 43-72 (`data/tic400170926/spoc_tces.csv`): eclipses every
  P/2 = 2.94788076 d from BTJD 2475.950533, duration 3.17 h (half-width 0.066 d). Both faint epochs lie
  **inside an eclipse window, in its first half**. Odd n and even n are the two different eclipses,
  so Gaia caught one of each.
- Their neighbours bracket them out of eclipse at normal brightness: 0.074 d earlier for both
  (offsets −0.135 and −0.118 d), and for the second also 0.176 d later (+0.132 d). For the second
  event, Gaia observed the star every 0.07-0.18 d over 2.5 days (times 2590.89-2593.40). That run
  holds exactly one predicted eclipse, and Gaia's single in-eclipse point is the faint one.
- Chance: each eclipse window covers 0.132 / 2.948 = 4.5% of the time, so two random faint epochs
  would both land in windows with probability about 0.2%.

**Reading:** the eclipses that TESS sees belong to HD 250735 itself. Gaia resolves stars 0.4″ apart,
while one TESS pixel is 21″. With CD-28 7251, two of our three new eclipsing binaries now have
Gaia's own photometry in eclipse at the times TESS predicts.

## An unexplained detail: the Gaia-era eclipses look early

1. Both faint epochs are dimmer than an ingress point should be. For a partial eclipse with the SPOC
   duration (our own a sin i ≈ 17.7 R_sun, R1+R2 ≈ 2 R_sun and i ≈ 85° give 3.2 h, matching SPOC), a point
   0.061 d before mid-eclipse is 92% of the way out to first contact, where the eclipse is only a few
   mmag deep. Gaia sees 37 mmag there. Both points would sit near mid-eclipse if the true eclipses in
   2015-2017 came about 0.02-0.04 d earlier than the TESS ephemeris extrapolated back about 795
   cycles.
2. Gaia's own double-lined orbit points the same way. For SB2C, t_periastron = −1.6427 ± 0.0102 d
   from 2016.0 (BTJD 389.0) is the ascending node of star 1. Its conjunction a quarter period later
   is BTJD 388.8313. That is **0.0196 d earlier** than eclipse n = −708 of the TESS ephemeris, about
   1.9σ from Gaia's t_periastron error alone.
3. Both readings would fit a true period slightly longer than SPOC's. Gaia's P/2 (2.9479675 ± 0.0000908 d) is longer
   than SPOC's by 8.7e-5 d, but at face value that would make the shift about 0.07 d, too much. A shift
   of about 0.03 d over 795 cycles needs a P/2 about 4e-5 d longer. TESS alone (sectors 43-72) should
   constrain the period better than that, unless the eclipse times vary (a third body, for example).

None of this is significant. It rests on two photometric points and a 1.9σ timing offset, and no error
was fitted to the SPOC ephemeris. It is a concrete, testable lead:
- sb2-new-ebs-timing-v1 (queued) measures the TESS period and times from all sectors (43-45, 71-73);
- Gaia DR4 (2 Dec) gives 66 months of epoch photometry and per-transit radial velocities. That data
  will show whether the Gaia-era eclipses really are early. Step g7
  (`findings/2026-11-24-g7-prereg.md`) fits the per-transit velocities with the conjunction time as
  a free parameter.

## Limits

Exploratory and by hand. Not the registered E4 grade: the job uses its own TESS times and window,
so its answer may differ. The eclipse window comes from SPOC's planet-model duration. Which eclipse
is primary is not yet known. The conversion of Gaia's t_periastron to a conjunction assumes Gaia's
standard convention (ω = 0 for circular orbits, v1 = γ + K1 cos(ν + ω)). This work was carried out
with AI agents and no human expert has checked it yet.
