## Short answer

TESS rules out **both** Gaia DR3 signals for this star:
- **Gaia's SB1 orbit** (P = 0.46023 d, K = 19.7 ± 1.3 km/s, e = 0.20) cannot be real. Any
  companion that produces it would raise tides on the Sun-like primary, and the TESS light
  curve shows none.
- **Gaia's photometric transit candidate** (P = 0.8514 d, 12.8 mmag, 3.2 h) is not in
  TESS: the box depth at that period is about 0.02%, against Gaia's ~1.2%.

So the "75 M_Jup companion transiting a Sun-like star every 11 hours" is withdrawn. This
is an honest negative.

## Data

- 14 TESS sectors (S15, 16, 22-25, 49-52, 76-78, 82), all TESS-SPOC, 42,731 points.
- The span is 1,848 d. Scatter per point is 1,200-4,500 ppm.

## Grades

| prediction | result | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: ≥ 10 TESS sectors | 14 sectors | **pass** |
| Q2: if H1 holds, the strongest Lomb-Scargle peak is at 0.2301 d | the strongest peaks are 3.82 d and 2.13 d at power ≈ 0.01, so there is no coherent signal | **fail**, and H1 is rejected |
| Q3: verdict probabilities (NO_ECLIPSE 25%, ...) | the script returned NO_ECLIPSE | see the bug below: the label is right, but the rule that produced it was broken |

**Bug (the same as in eblm_screen 0.1.0).** `vet_short_period.py` 0.1.0 calls astropy
BoxLeastSquares without flux errors. Its `depth_snr` is then in units of a flux error of 1,
so every "SNR" is about 0.01-0.03 and the detection gate (SNR ≥ 10) could never pass. The
NO_ECLIPSE label therefore came from the bug, not from the data. We count this as a fail
of the run. The label is supported by the direct measurements below, which do not use the
SNR. DR4 use needs `dy` passed to BLS (as `eblm_fit.py` already does).

## The measurements that decide it

**At the SB1 period** (harmonic fit outside eclipse, 22,146 points):
- Ellipsoidal semi-amplitude: a_ell = −26 ± 14 ppm.
- Prediction for an edge-on companion with m2 = 0.071 M☉: 9,426 ppm. The star fills 77% of
  its Roche lobe.
- Lower inclinations do not rescue the orbit. A lower inclination needs a heavier
  companion for the same mass function (f(M) = 3.43 × 10⁻⁴ M☉). We worked the minimum
  ellipsoidal amplitude for each companion mass by hand: Morris α = 1.30, R1 = 1.163 R☉
  (FLAME), M1 = 0.956 M☉, and a from Kepler's law.

| m2 (M☉) | sin i | a (R☉) | predicted ellipsoidal (ppm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.071 | 1.00 | 2.53 | 9,400 |
| 0.2 | 0.385 | 2.63 | 3,500 |
| 0.5 | 0.180 | 2.84 | 1,500 |
| 1.0 | 0.109 | 3.14 | 830 |
| 1.4 (white dwarf) | 0.089 | 3.34 | 630 |
| 3.0 (neutron star or black hole) | 0.058 | 3.97 | 350 |

Every case is excluded by more than 25σ. **The SB1 solution is spurious**: very probably
an alias or an artefact of 20 RVS epochs. A 0.46-d orbit with e = 0.20 around a G star
was already implausible, because tides circularise such orbits.

**Gaia's transit period.** The BLS depth at P_phot = 0.85108 d is 196 ppm (3.1 h box).
The deepest BLS box anywhere from 0.2 to 5 d is 0.18% (one 60-d block). Gaia's 12.8-mmag
transit would be ~60× deeper. The TIC contamination is 0.5%, so dilution cannot explain
the difference.

## What this changes

`findings/2026-11-17-dr3-transit-rv-screen.md` counted this star among the new DR3 transit
candidates that Gaia's own RVs show to carry a heavier-than-planet companion (9 of 41). With
its SB1 orbit shown to be spurious, the count is **8 of 41** (7 firm, 1 marginal). The
job's full DR3 screen reproduces the hand-made class counts:

The RV scatter of the RV-quiet stars has median 1.6 km/s and 90th percentile 3.7 km/s, so
the 7 km/s noise ceiling is conservative. The job's SIMBAD and VSX lookups all failed
(CDS TAP was unreachable).

**Lesson for DR4.** A short-period Gaia SB1 orbit with significant eccentricity, and no
photometric counterpart at P/2, should be treated as suspect until TESS shows the
ellipsoidal signal. The release-day screen should apply this ellipsoidal test to every
same-star SB1 claim at P < 2 d.
