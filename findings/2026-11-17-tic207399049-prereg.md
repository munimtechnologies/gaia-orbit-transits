## Why this star

- **Gaia photometry.** Gaia DR3 lists it as a new photometric transit candidate: P =
  0.85138 d, depth 12.8 mmag, 3.2 h, 18 points in transit.
- **Gaia RVS orbit.** Gaia DR3 also gives it an SB1 orbit:
  - P = 0.46023065 ± 0.0000059 d, K = 19.70 ± 1.31 km/s, e = 0.20;
  - significance 15.0;
  - rv_amplitude_robust = 38.6 km/s, consistent with 2K.
- **The star.** FLAME gives M1 = 0.956 M☉ and R1 = 1.163 R☉. Teff = 5711 K, G = 12.31,
  d ≈ 384 pc, RUWE 1.13, Tmag 11.90, TIC contamination 0.5%.
- **The companion.** m2 sin i = 0.0712 M☉ (74.6 M_Jup), with a 2σ range of 0.061–0.081
  M☉: at the brown-dwarf / star boundary.
- **Prior work.** ExoFOP has no TOI, no CTOI and no follow-up of any kind. A web search
  for the TIC, Gaia and 2MASS names found nothing.

**"Wow" if it holds.** A companion of about 75 M_Jup transiting a Sun-like star every 11
hours.
- As far as we found (a search on 2026-11-17, not a full literature review), every known
  transiting brown dwarf with P < 1 d orbits an M dwarf: NGTS-7Ab, TOI-263b and
  ZTF J2020+5033.
- It would be a benchmark for the mass-radius relation and for tides at the
  hydrogen-burning limit.

**Doubts, stated in advance.**
- Gaia's photometric period (0.8514 d) is not the SB1 period. Their frequencies differ by
  0.998 c/d.
- e = 0.20 is unexpected at 0.46 d.
- 20 RVS epochs can alias.
- TESS has observed this high-ecliptic-latitude star (β ≈ 74°) in many sectors, yet it
  never became a TOI. That fits an eclipsing-binary classification, or no signal.

## Expectations under H1 (SB1 correct, companion transits; sin i ≈ 1)

- a = 2.531 R☉ (a/R1 = 2.18). The star fills about 77% of its Roche lobe.
- Ellipsoidal semi-amplitude ≈ 0.94% (Morris α = 1.30), at P/2 = 0.2301 d.
- Central transit duration ≈ 1.9 h (0.078 d), for k ≈ 0.11.
- Depth ≈ 1.0–1.3%, so R2 ≈ 0.12–0.13 R☉.
- Secondary eclipse ≲ 0.1% (a 3,000 K body against 5,700 K in the TESS band: about 0.07%,
  plus reflection).

## Fixed decision rules (`verdict()`)

1. **Detection.** The strongest candidate period, found by BLS SNR among BLS-best,
   2×BLS, P_SB1, 2·P_SB1 and P_Gaia-phot, must have SNR ≥ 10 and a primary depth ≥ 3σ.
   Otherwise the verdict is **NO_ECLIPSE**.
   - *Half-period lock.* Before rule 2: if the odd and even depths at that period differ
     by > 3σ, the period is doubled (at most twice). This covers ellipsoidal minima or
     two eclipses folded together.
2. **Period.** If the eclipse period does not equal P_SB1 (1:1 within 0.1%), the verdict
   is **PERIOD_CONFLICT**.
3. **Twin binary.** If the odd and even depths differ by > 3σ, the verdict is
   **STELLAR_EB**.
4. **Luminous companion.** If secondary/primary ≥ 0.1, or R2 = R1·√depth ≥ 0.2 R☉, the
   verdict is **STELLAR_EB**.
5. **Mass check.** If the observed ellipsoidal amplitude is > 3 × predicted + 3σ, the
   verdict is **MASS_TENSION** (the companion is heavier than the SB1 says).
6. **Otherwise,** with m2 from K ± 2σ (sin i = 1):
   - **TRANSITING_BROWN_DWARF_CANDIDATE** if m2_hi < 0.075;
   - **TRANSITING_VLM_STAR** if m2_lo > 0.08;
   - else **TRANSITING_BD_OR_VLM_BOUNDARY**.

The depth errors include the error of the harmonic out-of-eclipse model extrapolated into
the eclipse.
