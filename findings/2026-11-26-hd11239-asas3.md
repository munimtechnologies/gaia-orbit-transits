## Status of this check
The rule (data cut, faint threshold, window, tests A1/A2) was written down in this run before any

## Result
- ASAS-3 (Pojmanski; star 014936-3944.5): 537 grade A/B points, HJD 2451868.6-2455158.6
  (Nov 2000 - Oct 2009). Baseline (median MAG_3) **V = 9.727**; faint = V >= 9.797: **22 points**.
- **20 of the 22 faint points lie within +-0.06 of an orbit of a conjunction of Gaia's
  double-lined orbit** (both windows cover 24% of the orbit, so about 5 would be expected by
  chance; binomial p = 5.5e-11). **A1 passes.**
- 11 are at conjunction 0 and 9 at conjunction 1, on 20 different nights over nine years.
  **A2 passes.**
- Robustness: five faint points are more than 0.2 mag below the baseline (0.23-1.07 mag), deeper
  than any eclipse of this pair can be (Gaia's deepest epoch, 0.003 of an orbit from conjunction,
  is 0.086 mag), so they are probably bad frames. Without them, **17 of 17** faint points are in
  a window. The two faint points outside the windows are both among these five.
- The phase offsets are tight: 15 of those 17 lie within 0.017 of an orbit of a conjunction;
  median offset -0.0067 of an orbit (about 30 minutes early). The ASAS epochs are 715-1,769
  orbits before Gaia's reference conjunction, where Gaia's period error alone allows a 1-sigma
  drift of 0.009-0.022 of an orbit, so the offset is within the ephemeris uncertainty.
  (The rule file says "about 1,100-1,780 orbits"; the correct lower end is 715. The window was
  set by the upper end, so the slip does not change the rule.)
- Depth: the faint points near conjunction are 0.07-0.14 mag below the baseline, matching
  Gaia's single-epoch depths (0.04-0.09 mag in G, BP and RP).

## What this means
Two independent instruments, nine years apart (ASAS-3 V band 2000-2009; Gaia G, BP, RP
2014-2017), see HD 11239 fainter at the same two conjunctions of Gaia's double-lined orbit. Gaia
resolves no neighbour within 21.6" and sees the dips in its sub-arcsecond windows, so the
eclipses belong to the F8 + F8 pair (minimum masses 1.09 and 1.05 Msun, P = 3.1208 d).
**HD 11239 is a double-lined eclipsing binary, found from Gaia data alone** (rule g8) and
confirmed in archival ASAS-3 photometry. The pre-registered TESS test (hd11239-tess-v1) is still
queued; it will give the light curve and inclination needed for masses and radii.

## Limits
- ASAS-3 has 15" pixels and ~0.03 mag scatter; it confirms timing, not depth or shape.
- The data CSV was transcribed through a fetch tool, not downloaded byte for byte; anyone can
  re-fetch the original from the URL in hd11239_asas3.py.
- HJD (ASAS) and BJD_TCB (Gaia) differ by less than 0.002 d (< 0.001 of an orbit); ignored.
- Made with AI agents; no human expert has checked it.
