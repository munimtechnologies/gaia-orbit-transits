What it means, as far as the pre-registration lets us say:
- **The eclipse is real and belongs to the Gaia SB1 system.** It repeats at the Gaia period
  with equal odd and even depths, and the shape implies a primary of the size Gaia's FLAME
  module gives independently (r1_ratio 0.99).
- **The companion's fitted radius is about twice the main-sequence radius for the mass that
  Gaia's orbit gives.** The eclipse is too deep for any 0.1 M☉ main-sequence star: even at
  b = 0, the depth needs k ≈ 0.22 (R₂ ≈ 0.20 R☉). A third light would make the true eclipse
  deeper still, not shallower.
- The occultation temperature (≈ 2950 K) is what a late-M dwarf of this size would show. It
  does not trip eblm_fit's "too luminous" test (allowed maximum 2907 K × 1.15).

**Under the pre-registration we do not call the companion "inflated" yet.** Two checks come
first:
1. **Is the system young?** A pre-main-sequence 0.1 M☉ star is this large at a few tens of
   Myr. A kinematic test (BANYAN Σ, with an AU Mic control) is now pre-registered in
   `findings/2026-11-19-tic71945261-followup-prereg.md` and queued as
   gaia-tic71945261-followup-v1.
2. **Is Gaia's K right?** A normal-size companion would need about 0.21 M☉, which would need
   K ≈ 32 km/s, twice Gaia's value. Two facts already in hand argue against that, but neither
   was pre-registered, so they are reported, not graded:
   - Gaia DR3's `rv_amplitude_robust` (the spread of the 15 epoch RVs after outlier
     rejection) is **32.5 km/s**. That matches 2K = 32.3 km/s. With K = 32 km/s, the spread
     would be expected near 64 km/s.
   - The ellipsoidal amplitude is −198 ± 206 ppm. A 0.21 M☉ companion predicts about
     660 ppm, which is 4σ away. The 0.097 M☉ companion predicts 304 ppm, which is 2.4σ
     away. This comes from one QLP sector with visible systematics, so it is weak evidence.
   The follow-up job also searches the archives (RAVE DR6, GALAH DR3, APOGEE DR17) for an
   independent RV epoch.

## The comparison system failed its prediction

We predicted **C** (a normal companion) for TOI-1251.01 and got **D_TWIN_AT_2P**: its odd and
even eclipses differ in depth (1.088 vs 0.921, ± 0.017 each), as if two similar stars orbited
at twice the period. The pre-registration only made TIC 71945261's label uninterpretable if
the comparison came out A or B, so TIC 71945261's label stands. But the prediction was wrong,
and we should have seen why beforehand:
- the fit table itself shows that TOI-1251 (TIC 233120979) is **in the TESS EB catalogue**
  (Prša et al. 2022) at P = 5.96309 d;
- ExoFOP lists it as TFOP "APC/SB1; Gaia SB1 at this period", with a stellar companion
  0.21″ away (Δmag ≈ 1.4-1.7 in speckle imaging), and an uploaded odd/even and 2P analysis.

We make no claim about TOI-1251. A comparison system has to be checked against the EB
catalogues first, like a candidate; that rule is now in our checklist.
