# Pre-registration: which star makes TIC 93622019's transit, and does it show binary signs?

Written 2026-11-22 (~16:00 UTC) by MV2 of Munim, Inc.; made with AI agents working in his name,
not checked by a human expert. Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0.
Code: `pixel_which_star.py` 0.1.0 with tests `test_pixel_which_star.py`; inputs
`runbook/pixel/targets.json`. The PSF and difference-image core is imported unchanged from
`tyc_pixel.py` 0.1.0 (commit c16e7a2a6a13cce476f73153483cc59ad8443362), which ran on real TESS
pixels in job tyc6340-pixel-v2 (graded today, `findings/2026-11-22-tyc6340-pixel-results.md`).
Job: `tic93622019-pixel-v1`. **Nobody has looked at the TESS pixels of TIC 93622019 or of the
controls.** This document is fixed before the job runs.

## Why

TIC 93622019 (Gaia DR3 5345417757181174144, G 12.57, F star, about 590 pc) shows a
flat-bottomed, roughly 1% transit every 2.123885 d in all six TESS sectors
(`findings/2026-11-22-vpt-pf-dr3-results.md`). Gaia DR3 lists it as a transit candidate at
2.83166 d, a 4:3 alias. Gaia's own photometry already sees the dip on this source, so no star
that Gaia resolves can cause the dip *in Gaia's data*. The TESS signal still needs its own
test: TESS pixels are 21″ wide, the TIC lists a contamination ratio of 0.187, and a G = 8.89
star lies 100″ away. Two warning signs from the light curve also need errors that include red
noise: a possible secondary eclipse (277 ± 71 ppm, 3.9σ with white-noise errors) and a 2.7σ
odd/even difference. This test decides whether the candidate goes to ExoFOP as a community TOI.

## Gaia stars near the target (Gaia DR3, queried 2026-11-22)

Within 60″, the stars bright enough to make the dip are G 16.67 at 17.7″, G 16.30 at 19.8″,
G 14.29 at 40.6″, G 15.76 at 50.0″, G 14.66 at 55.0″, G 15.25 at 56.6″ and fainter stars.
Gaia lists nothing within 17.6″ brighter than G 19 except the target, whose RUWE is 0.90.

## Method (fixed)

For each of TESS sectors 10, 37, 64, 90, 99 and 100 (TESScut 15 × 15 FFI cutouts):
1. **Ephemeris.** 3 × 3 aperture on the target. Detrend by medians of 0.5-day bins with the
   transits masked (±(D/2 + 0.08 d), D = 0.125 d). Fit a trapezoid with an ingress of 0.12 D
   on a ±0.06 d grid for each sector at a fixed P. Sector epochs with Δχ² ≥ 25 enter one
   weighted linear ephemeris. If its period differs from 2.1238848 d by more than 0.002 d, it
   is rejected and only the mean shift is kept.
2. **Pixels.** A PSF model (Gaussian core and halo) of the mean out-of-transit image includes
   every Gaia DR3 star with RP < 17, with free fluxes for RP < 14. Each transit gives one
   difference image: the mean of the out-of-transit windows (0.6-1.4 D on both sides) minus
   the in-transit mean (|Δt| ≤ 0.4 D). Neighbour N for the two-source amplitude test is the
   brightest star 1-3 px from the target that could make the dip.
3. **Controls in each sector (as in tyc_pixel):** C1 nulls at phases 0.25 and 0.75
   (|z| < 3); C2 a 10% dip injected on N must come out as PIX_ON_NEIGHBOUR and
   CENTROID_ON_NEIGHBOUR; C3 a dip of the reference depth (0.883%) injected on the target must
   come out as PIX_ON_TARGET and CENTROID_ON_TARGET. A sector is **sensitive** only if C1-C3
   all pass.
4. **Centroid on the sky.** A free-position PSF fit of the mean difference image (300
   amplitude and 100 position bootstraps over transits; a systematic floor from isolated bright
   stars, 0.15 px if fewer than 3) gives the offset of the transit signal from the target in
   arcsec, east and north. These offsets are combined over sensitive sectors by inverse
   variance. The error is inflated by √χ²_red when the sectors disagree.

## Labels

- **WS (primary).** Compare the combined offset with the target and with every Gaia star within
  120″ that is capable of the dip, i.e. its RP flux ratio is at least 0.5 × 0.883%.
  **WS_ON_TARGET**: within 3σ of the target and more than 3σ from every capable star.
  **WS_UNRESOLVED**: within 3σ of the target and of at least one capable star.
  **WS_OFF_TARGET**: more than 3σ from the target. **WS_UNTESTABLE**: fewer than 2 sensitive
  sectors.
- **DEPTH:** the PSF-deblended in-transit depth, inverse-variance mean over sensitive sectors,
  against the flat-bottom depth 1.05%. CONSISTENT if the ratio is within 0.85-1.15 or within
  2σ; otherwise SHALLOWER or DEEPER. (The in-window mean reaches slightly into ingress, by
  about 1%.)
- **SEC:** event-by-event depths at phase 0.5 from the aperture light curve, divided by the
  target's modelled flux fraction in the aperture. The error is the event-to-event scatter
  divided by √N, which includes red noise on the transit timescale. The same is done at
  phases 0.25 and 0.75 (nulls). **SEC_UNRELIABLE** if either null has |z| ≥ 3; otherwise
  **SEC_DETECTED** if z ≥ 3, else **SEC_NOT_DETECTED**, with a 3σ upper limit.
- **OE:** odd against even transit depths, same method; **OE_DIFFER** if |z| ≥ 3.
- **EB:** **EB_SUSPECT** if OE_DIFFER, or if SEC_DETECTED and the secondary's lower 2σ bound
  exceeds 150 ppm. A hot Jupiter around this F star should give at most about 100 ppm in the
  TESS band, from heat plus reflected light. Otherwise **EB_NOT_INDICATED**.
- Reported, not graded: the companion radius from the deblended depth, √δ × R* with
  R* = 1.36-1.41 R_sun, and the refined ephemeris.

## Controls (chosen by rule before any pixel was seen)

Rule: the two confirmed transiting planets in the NASA Exoplanet Archive pscomppars table
(queried 2026-11-22) with TESS magnitude 11.2-13.2, depth 0.5-2.0%, period 1-5 d, RA 140-210°
and Dec −70° to −35° that are nearest on the sky to the target. These are **HATS-67 b** (7.2°
away) and **HATS-58 A b** (7.9°). Each control uses its 3 newest TESS sectors and the
archive's ephemeris at run time.

**CONTROLS_PASS** requires both controls to come out WS_ON_TARGET or WS_UNRESOLVED, i.e. the
method must not push a real on-target transit off its host. This criterion was chosen after
learning, before any pixel was seen, that HATS-58 A has a companion 0.74″ away, only 0.9 mag
fainter in G and nearly equal in RP (Espinoza et al. 2019, AJ 158, 63). Gaia DR3 lists it.
No TESS centroid can separate that pair, so this control is expected to be UNRESOLVED.
HATS-67 has capable Gaia neighbours 9.8″ and 11.6″ away. If the controls fail, the target's
labels are reported but not graded.

Not blind: we know the target's light curve, and that the controls are real planets.

## Predictions

- TIC 93622019: WS_ON_TARGET 80%, WS_UNRESOLVED 10%, WS_OFF_TARGET 5%, WS_UNTESTABLE 5%.
- CONTROLS_PASS 85%. HATS-58 A b WS_UNRESOLVED 85%; HATS-67 b WS_ON_TARGET 50%.
- At least 5 of the 6 target sectors sensitive: 70%.
- DEPTH_CONSISTENT 60%, DEEPER 25%, SHALLOWER 15%.
- SEC_DETECTED 30% (the 3.9σ white-noise hint should weaken with red-noise errors).
- OE_CONSISTENT 85%.
- EB_NOT_INDICATED 70%.

## What follows from each outcome

- **WS_ON_TARGET and EB_NOT_INDICATED:** submit TIC 93622019 to ExoFOP as a community TOI
  once MV2 has an ExoFOP account, so observers with spectrographs can measure its mass. Still
  called a candidate, not a planet.
- **WS_ON_TARGET and EB_SUSPECT:** report it as a likely eclipsing binary on the target. The
  candidate is not submitted as a planet candidate.
- **WS_UNRESOLVED:** name the stars not excluded and the flux each would need to lose. Gaia's
  own photometry still decides for stars Gaia resolves.
- **WS_OFF_TARGET:** the TESS signal comes from another star; withdraw the candidate and say so
  wherever it was published.
- The same module, unchanged, will vet the period-free survivors of Gaia DR4's transit
  candidates after 2 December.

## Limits

The PSF is Gaussian, not the TESS PRF, and the injections use the same model. Stars closer than
about one TESS pixel are separated only by the centroid. Sector offsets are treated as
independent. The secondary-eclipse test uses a fixed phase of 0.5, so an eccentric orbit
would be missed. Gaia RP stands in for the TESS band.
