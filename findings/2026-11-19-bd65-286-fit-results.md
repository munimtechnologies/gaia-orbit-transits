**The pre-registration says: "TOI-1251.01: C ... If it comes out otherwise, the other two
labels are not interpreted."** The control came out D. So, under our own rule, **BD+65 286's
label C is not graded as a result**, even though every number we predicted for it came out
inside its window. We keep to the rule.

Why the control failed is already known and is not a fault of BD+65 286's fit. TOI-1251
(TIC 233120979) is in the TESS EB catalogue at 5.963 d, and TFOP lists a stellar companion
0.21″ away; its odd and even eclipse depths differ (1.088 ± 0.017 vs 0.921 ± 0.017). We had
seen that at 11:30 UTC in the TIC 71945261 job, which ran the same control and got exactly the
same numbers, but this pre-registration had been written at 08:40 with the stricter rule. The
control was a bad choice, and the lesson ("vet a comparison system like a candidate") is
already in STATUS. A rule cannot be relaxed after the outcome is known, so the fix is a new,
properly chosen control set, pre-registered now: `findings/2026-11-19-eblm-control-prereg.md`
(job gaia-eblmctl-v1). Its controls are six published systems whose masses and radii were
measured independently (Swayne et al. 2024, CHEOPS + TESS).

What can be said without the label:
- **The eclipse is in the public TESS data at Gaia's period**, in all eight sectors from 2019
  to 2025, with equal odd and even eclipses and an occultation. That is an observation, not an
  interpretation; NASA's SPOC pipeline also detected the dip in sector 52 (TCE, 2022).
- The ellipsoidal amplitude measured in the same fit is 0.81 of what Gaia's mass predicts. That
  is an independent photometric check of Gaia's mass, inside the window we set.
- No claim is made here that the companion is a "normal" or an "inflated" star. If the new
  control set passes, the label C stands as measured above; if it fails, it is not used.

(`data/bd65286-v1/fit_plots/fit_1802445358630815104.png`). Possible readings include a true
period twice Gaia's, an eclipsing body unrelated to the Gaia orbit, or uneven coverage. Under
the control rule we do not interpret it, and we make no statement about the TFOP or RAVEN calls
for this TOI.

## Limits
- BD+65 286's companion mass rests on Gaia's single-lined orbit and the FLAME primary mass. The
  light-curve and FLAME primary radii differ by 7%, which moves the inflation from +1% to +9%.
- eblm_fit's main-sequence radius table is a simple interpolation, not a full isochrone; it
  does not model irradiation of the companion by its 6,300 K primary at a/R₁ = 3.4.
- One pre-registered control failed, so the BD+65 286 label is not graded until the new
  control set has run.
- The work was carried out with AI agents and no human expert has checked it yet.
